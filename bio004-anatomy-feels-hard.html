Learning anatomy · BIO 004 · Fall 2026

Anatomy is hard. The struggle is not a sign you are failing.

You are learning hundreds of new words and finding each structure in three dimensions, packed in with everything around it. It is hard for almost everyone, especially in the first few weeks. Here is what the struggle means and what to do with it.

Why it feels this way

Some struggle is the learning itself.

01Desirable difficulty.When studying takes more effort in the moment, what you learn sticks better and lasts longer. Learning researchers have a name for it.
02Familiar is not the same as known.Rereading makes material feel familiar, and familiar feels like knowing it, even when you cannot pull it back out on an exam.
03Harder methods work better.Pulling an answer from memory, spreading study over days, and mixing topics all feel harder than rereading. They also work better.
The research

Rereading feels better. Recall works better.

In one well-known study, one group of students kept rereading a passage and another group practiced recalling it from memory. A week later:

01 · Practiced recalling61%remembered a week later by the students who practiced pulling it from memory.
02 · Kept rereading40%remembered by the students who kept rereading. They also felt more confident that they had learned it.
A large review of study methods found the same thing. Practice testing and spacing your study out over time came out on top. Rereading and highlighting came out near the bottom.Roediger and Karpicke · 2006
Dunlosky and colleagues · 2013

What this means for how you study

Pull it out of your memory, a little every day.

  1. 01
    Redraw your note sheets from memory.The way you do in a brain dump. Then check them against your notes and fix what you missed.
  2. 02
    Practice brain dumps before you look at your notes.Reaching first and checking second is what makes it stick.
  3. 03
    Use your recall cards and Mastery OS a little every day.Not all at once. Spreading it out is the whole point.
  4. 04
    Explain answers out loud to your team during TBLs.Each of these feels harder than rereading. That is the point, and it is why they work.
