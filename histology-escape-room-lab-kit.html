Instructor lab kit

Outbreak: Ward 4

Histology escape room for lecture and lab. Ninety minutes, eight stations, three microscope benches. Students never name the organism. They read tissue damage, build a tropism map, and use it to find the route of transmission and the source.

Instructor passcode 4004

Part 1

Solving the microscope bottleneck

Eight teams all reaching for Bench A at minute twelve is the fastest way to kill this activity. The game is built so that never happens. There are two models. Pick one before you print anything.

Model 1: shared benches, teams routed

Act two of the game is three independent investigation tracks. Each track is one screen station plus one bench, and the three tracks produce three independent pieces of evidence, so a team can meet them in any order and still reach the same conclusion at station eight. Each team picks a route on the setup screen, and the route decides which bench they visit first.

Route 1Airway first
  1. Secretion Log, screen
  2. Epithelial Ward, Bench A
  3. Cell Registry, screen
  4. Connective Vault, Bench B
  5. Skin Lab, Bench C
Route 2Framework first
  1. Cell Registry, screen
  2. Connective Vault, Bench B
  3. Skin Lab, Bench C
  4. Secretion Log, screen
  5. Epithelial Ward, Bench A
Route 3Barrier first
  1. Skin Lab, Bench C
  2. Secretion Log, screen
  3. Epithelial Ward, Bench A
  4. Cell Registry, screen
  5. Connective Vault, Bench B

Hand out routes as teams sit down: first team gets Route 1, second gets Route 2, third gets Route 3, then start over. With nine teams that is three teams per route, and a bench never has more than three teams pointed at it at the same moment. The route number shows in the status bar all game so you can see at a glance where a team is headed.

Scale it to your bench count. Three benches is the minimum. If you can set up two of each bench, six benches total, run the same routes and the queue disappears entirely. Bench C is only two slides, so it clears fastest; if you can duplicate only one bench, duplicate Bench A.

Model 2: every team has its own tray

If you have a scope at every team table and enough slides for a full set per team, this is the better run. Switch Bench model to own tray in the instructor panel and every team runs the same fixed order with no routing at all. No walking, no queue, no rotation to explain.

How the patient specimen works

Your teaching slides are normal tissue, and they should be. Students identify the real tissue on the real slide, which is the assessed skill. The story overlay lives on the bench card. Each card carries a short patient specimen report the team reads only after they have identified the slides, and the direct observation question in the app is answered from that report. Nothing asks a student to see damage that is not on the slide.

The facility is fictional

Carquinez Regional Medical Center does not exist. The name is local enough to feel real to a Solano student without naming an actual hospital as the site of a fatal outbreak caused by facility negligence. A disclaimer appears in the student opening. If you ever swap in a real facility name, expect a screenshot of it to travel further than you intend.

Part 3

Running the ninety minutes

What students see

The game opens like a title sequence. Lines fade onto a dark screen one at a time, six beats, each with a Next button, so nobody faces a wall of text. Every station opens the same way with its own case beat before any question appears. Then questions come one at a time, checked one at a time, with a progress rail across the top. A wrong answer holds the team on that item until they fix it. Every station ends with an evidence reveal that logs to their field notebook.

Any beat can be flattened instantly with the Show all text button in the corner, which also happens automatically for anyone browsing with reduced motion turned on.

Timeline for a ninety minute session
ClockWhat happens
0 to 8 minTeams sit, open the file, watch the opening beats, name themselves, enter their route number. Their clock starts when they press Start.
8 to 22 minAct one, Intake Bay and Triage Board. No benches in play. Everyone is on screen, so the room is quiet and you can circulate.
22 to 72 minAct two. Teams disperse by route. Watch for a bench with more than three teams waiting and pull the next team forward to their screen station instead.
72 to 82 minAct three, Outbreak Board. Teams work from their field notebook. This is the payoff. Do not help.
82 to 90 minDebrief questions from the finale screen, out loud, whole room.

Team roles, four students

  • Driver. Sits at the keyboard. Enters nothing the team has not agreed on out loud.
  • Scope lead. Owns the bench trips and calls the identification before anyone checks the cipher.
  • Recorder. Keeps the paper tropism map running as evidence comes in.
  • Skeptic. Has to say why an answer is wrong before the driver types it. Rotates every two stations.

Instructor panel

Click Instructor in the status bar, passcode 4004. You can set the clock, switch between the routed and own tray bench models, change a team's route mid-game, switch microscope gates off entirely for a screen only run, and unlock a team through any station. Unlocking reloads that station's evidence into the field notebook, so a team restored after an accidental refresh can still finish station eight.

Setup checklist

  • Bench model decided, Model 1 or Model 2, and the instructor panel set to match.
  • Scopes focused on their assigned slide before students arrive, low power, specimen centered.
  • Bench cards cut, sorted and placed at the correct bench.
  • Cipher poster at every bench and one per team table.
  • Route cards 1, 2, 3 dealt out in order as teams sit, Model 1 only.
  • Escape room file open on every team computer, opening screen showing.
  • Instructor panel tested once, passcode confirmed.

Part 6

Answer key

Instructor copy. Do not post. The app grades everything automatically, so this is for circulating, for coaching, and for the paper version. Stations are listed in content order; teams meet them in route order.

Intake Bay, act one

  1. 400x
  2. Parfocal
  3. Field of view, working distance, and amount of light reaching your eye all decrease
  4. Close the iris diaphragm slightly
  5. Scanning objective, coarse adjustment, center the structure, rotate to the next objective, fine adjustment only

Triage Board, act one

Mind map. Epithelial: free apical surface with a basement membrane; avascular, fed by diffusion; regenerates rapidly and is the origin of most carcinomas. Connective: few cells in abundant matrix; fibers and ground substance built by fibroblasts; includes the only fluid tissue. Muscle: elongated cells packed with actin and myosin; three varieties, one voluntary and two involuntary. Nervous: cells with long conducting processes; supported by neuroglia.

  1. Polarity, with an apical surface and a basal surface anchored to a basement membrane

Secretion Log, airway track

Glands. Endocrine, ductless into blood. Exocrine, duct onto a surface. Merocrine, exocytosis with the cell intact. Apocrine, apical portion pinches off. Holocrine, whole cell ruptures. Goblet cell, unicellular exocrine gland secreting mucin.

Membranes. Serous, parietal and visceral layers around a closed cavity. Mucous, lines cavities open to the exterior, has a lamina propria. Cutaneous, the skin. Synovial, dense connective tissue only, no epithelium.

  1. Sebaceous gland
  2. Loss of the mucus layer that traps inhaled particles
  3. Goblet cells and mucous glands supply the mucus; cilia beat toward the pharynx; losing it lets particles travel deeper

Epithelial Ward, airway track, Bench A

Matching. Simple squamous to alveoli and endothelium and serous membranes. Simple cuboidal to kidney tubules, thyroid follicles and small ducts. Simple columnar to stomach and intestine. Pseudostratified ciliated columnar to trachea and upper airway. Stratified squamous nonkeratinized to esophagus, oral cavity and vagina. Stratified squamous keratinized to epidermis. Transitional to bladder and ureters. Stratified cuboidal to sweat and larger salivary gland ducts.

Order, thinnest to thickest barrier. Simple squamous, simple cuboidal, pseudostratified ciliated columnar, stratified squamous nonkeratinized, stratified squamous keratinized.

  1. The cells at the free apical surface
  2. Volume change without tearing the lining
  3. Simple squamous and simple cuboidal in the kidney tubule

Bench A codes: A1 = 14, A2 = 11, A3 = 12, A4 = 15. Observation: cilia blunted and stripped, alveolar walls thickened with air spaces flooded.

Cell Registry, framework track

Connective and bone cells. Fibroblast builds fibers and ground substance. Chondrocyte is the mature cartilage cell in a lacuna. Osteocyte is the mature bone cell in a lacuna with canaliculi. Osteoblast deposits matrix. Osteoclast dissolves matrix. Adipocyte stores triglyceride.

Defense and secretion cells. Goblet cell secretes mucin. Mast cell releases histamine and heparin. Macrophage is the tissue phagocyte from a monocyte. Plasma cell makes antibody. Neutrophil is the first responder phagocyte.

  1. Alveolar macrophages
  2. Histamine increases capillary permeability, so fluid leaves the vessels
  3. Fibroblast, macrophage, mast cell

Connective Vault, framework track, Bench B

Mind map. Connective tissue proper: areolar, adipose, reticular, dense regular, dense irregular, elastic connective. Supporting: hyaline cartilage, elastic cartilage, fibrocartilage, compact bone. Fluid: blood.

Proper. Areolar packs and does immune surveillance. Adipose stores and insulates. Reticular forms lymphoid stroma. Dense regular resists tension in one direction. Dense irregular resists tension from many directions. Elastic connective recoils in large artery walls.

Supporting and fluid. Hyaline on articular surfaces and in respiratory passages. Elastic in external ear and epiglottis. Fibrocartilage in discs and menisci. Compact bone as osteons. Blood transports in a liquid matrix.

Order, most fluid to hardest. Blood, areolar, dense regular, hyaline cartilage, fibrocartilage, compact bone.

  1. It is avascular, so cells rely on diffusion through the matrix

Bench B codes: B1 = 24, B2 = 27, B3 = 30, B4 = 31. Observation: tendon, cartilage and bone normal; blood shows a sharp neutrophil rise.

Skin Lab, barrier track, Bench C

Strata, deep to superficial. Basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum, corneum.

Mind map. Epidermis: keratinocyte, melanocyte, tactile (Merkel) cell, dendritic (Langerhans) cell, keratohyaline granules. Dermis: dermal papillae, tactile (Meissner) corpuscle, hair follicle, arrector pili, sebaceous gland, eccrine sweat gland. Hypodermis: adipose tissue, lamellar (Pacinian) corpuscle.

Epidermal structures. Melanocyte makes pigment. Merkel cell is a touch receptor of the stratum basale. Langerhans cell is the antigen presenting immune cell. Dermal papillae interlock with epidermal ridges.

Dermal and hypodermal structures. Sebaceous gland is holocrine. Eccrine sweat gland is merocrine. Arrector pili is smooth muscle. Meissner corpuscle senses fine touch. Pacinian corpuscle senses deep pressure and vibration. Reticular layer is dense irregular connective tissue.

  1. Stratum lucidum
  2. Cells pushed superficially from the basale; keratin accumulates and organelles are lost; deep keratinocytes fed by diffusion from dermal papillae

Bench C codes: C1 = 16, C2 = 22. Observation: epidermis intact through all five strata, no breach and no infiltrate.

Outbreak Board, act three

Tropism map. Damaged: pseudostratified ciliated columnar, alveolar simple squamous, goblet cells. Spared: keratinized stratified squamous epidermis, dense regular connective tissue, simple cuboidal of the kidney tubule, nonkeratinized stratified squamous of the esophagus, compact bone.

  1. Inhalation of a fine aerosol small enough to reach the alveoli
  2. Droplets are too large and deposit in the upper airway before reaching the alveoli
  3. Sat within six meters of the West Atrium fountain, attack rate 42 percent
  4. Strengthens it, because her exposure was to the same water system by a different path
  5. Shut down, drain and disinfect the West Atrium fountain and its recirculation system

Part 7

Notes and extensions

What the game actually assesses

Identification, location, function, special cells, and ordering, which is exactly the lab practical skill set. The outbreak narrative is the reason to care, not the content. No microbiology knowledge is required or rewarded, and no student is asked to name a pathogen.

Where it fits

Module 1, after histology and integumentary have been taught. It works as a review before Exam 1, or as the lab session that follows epithelial and connective tissue identification. It is not a first exposure activity.

Running it screen only

Switch the microscope gates off in the instructor panel and all eight stations become screen work with no benches. Useful for a make up session or an online section.

Second run, same course

The cipher codes are the only thing a student could carry from one section to another. Change the assignments in one place, the CIPHER object at the top of the escape room file, and reprint the poster. Everything regrades automatically.
