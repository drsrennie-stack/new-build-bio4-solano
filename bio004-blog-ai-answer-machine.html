AI Is Not an Answer Machine

Dr. Sharilyn Rennie · Aug 24, 2026 · 3 min read

Most people use AI like a vending machine. Question in, answer out.

In a class like this one, that is the worst possible way to use it.

Not because the answer is wrong, although sometimes it is. Because the answer appears on your screen instead of coming out of your head. You read it, it makes sense, and it feels like progress. It is the rereading trap with better manners. Somebody else did the retrieval, and retrieval was the whole workout.

If AI produces the answer, you learned almost nothing. If AI produces the tool and you produce the answers, now you are getting somewhere.

AI is a workshop, not an oracle

Here is what most students have not discovered yet. You can describe a learning problem to AI and have it build you a tool that attacks exactly that problem. Not a generic app built for some imaginary average student. A tool built for you.

Keep confusing the epithelium look-alikes? Ask for a matching game with exactly those tissues, the ones you personally mix up, that shuffles them and makes you choose.

Have ADHD, or attention that comes in bursts? Ask for a quiz game with five minute rounds, a timer, points, and a streak counter, so the practice fits the way your focus actually works instead of demanding a brain you don't have. You know how you learn best. AI can build to your spec.

Want spaced practice but hate every flashcard app you have tried? Describe the app you wish existed and watch it get built in front of you.

This is not hypothetical. Most of the tools in this course, the tissue chart, the recall cards, the loops, started as a sentence that began "build me a." You are living inside the proof that this works.

The one rule

Whatever you build, point it in one direction: the tool asks, you answer. Ask AI to quiz you, not to tell you. Ask it to hide the answers, not to hand them over. Ask it to check your explanation after you give one, not to explain first.

Don't ask AI to be your brain. Ask it to build the gym your brain trains in.

One honest warning. AI sounds confident even when it is wrong, and anatomy has a thousand details worth being right about. Check what it builds against your notes and your textbook. That checking is not wasted time. It is one more round of retrieval, and it makes you the expert in the room instead of the passenger.

The students who will thrive with AI are not the ones who ask it the most questions. They are the ones who make it build the best tools, and then do the work themselves inside them.
