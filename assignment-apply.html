    What a strong answer looks like

    Length is not what makes it strong. A good answer to any of these fits on one page and does four things.

    • It names the thing precisely. Not "blood pressure" but "mean arterial pressure", not "the enzyme" but which enzyme and where it works. Then it says why that distinction matters here.
    • It traces the mechanism in order rather than jumping from stimulus to outcome. Every step gets named, including the ones that are easy to skip.
    • It separates what the evidence shows from what you are inferring. Both are allowed. Confusing them is not.
    • It names what would change the answer. The most useful sentence in a clinical write up starts "I would want to know".

    A correct number with no explanation earns half credit. That is true every week, on every question.

    Using AI on this one

    Open, with disclosure. Use It is the category where you are allowed to use AI, and you have to say so.

    • Use it to check a definition, to ask how a mechanism works, or to push back on your own reasoning.
    • At the end of your answer, add one or two lines: what you used it for, and what you did with what it gave you. "I asked it to explain the Haldane effect and then rewrote question three because my first version had the direction backwards" is a good disclosure.
    • What is being graded is whether your naming, your mechanism and your interpretation hold together as one person thinking. That is very visible when it is missing.

    Undisclosed use is the only version of this that is a problem.

    Turning it in

    1. Say which case you chose at the top of the page, by its letter and its title. If you do not name it, it cannot be graded.
    2. Answer all five questions in order, numbered. About one page. Handwritten is fine and always has been.
    3. Add your AI disclosure at the end, even if it is one line saying you did not use any.
    4. Photograph or scan into one PDF, in order, and submit it here before 10:00 pm. Wait for the Canvas confirmation screen.
