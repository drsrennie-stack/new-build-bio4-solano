BIO 004 · Human Anatomy · Study Protocol

This is what you should try

If week 1 did not go the way you wanted, do not just read the notes again harder. Pick one method and run it for a week. This page is a flow chart, like a clinical protocol: start at the top, do one box at a time, in order. Every box that has a tool links straight to it.

What is coming at you first?

Both lanes work. Start with the one that worries you more.

The Cell TBL · lecture material The tissue lab · lab material

Lecture laneDo well on the Cell TBL

Goal: walk in having already recalled every high-yield cell concept from memory, more than once.

  1. Step 1

    Open the competencies

    Find the Cell Anatomy section and filter to the high-yield items. That list is your target list, and it is the whole game: it is what a question can be written about.

    Open the competency checklist PDF version ↗

  2. Step 2 · paper and pencil

    Build a study box

    Plain sheet of paper. Draw a box. Label it with ONE high-yield topic. Inside the box: no full sentences. A tiny mind map, a drawing, a ladder, arrows. Only things a question could ask.

  3. Step 3 · paper and pencil

    Next high-yield, next box

    Keep going until you have a sheet, or sheets, of big squares. When you finish, you have pre-studied the entire topic. That is what pre-studied means.

  4. Step 4

    Go back to the pre-work

    Open your cell pre-work and see if you can answer those questions now, after the boxes. The ones you miss go back into a box.

    Open the Cell pre-work

  5. Step 5

    Watch the cell videos, boxes beside you

    Have your boxes ready. When lecture adds a detail your box is missing, add it to the box right then.

    Watch the Cell videos

  6. Step 6 · paper and pencil

    Recall the boxes from memory

    Fresh blank page. Rebuild one box without looking. Check it against the original. Fix what you missed. Do that over and over. This step is the studying; everything before it was setup.

  7. Step 7

    Recall cards: Cell first, then loop back

    Do the cards on the Cell. Then go back and review the Intro to Anatomy cards, and keep coming back to older cards every couple of days. You are not done with old material just because class moved on.

    Open the recall cards

Lab laneDo well on the tissue lab

Goal: recognize every tissue type on sight and recall its location, function, and special structures from memory.

  1. Step 1 · paper and pencil

    Build your tissue chart

    Make a study sheet: one row per tissue type. Put a picture of the tissue in it if you can. Columns for everything a lab exam can ask: location, function, special structures and what they do, anything called out as special.

    Pull from the Tissues notes

  2. Step 2 · paper and pencil

    Recall the chart from memory

    Blank copy of the chart. Fill it in without looking. Check, fix, repeat. As the rows get easy, cover the picture and name the tissue from its description, then flip it and do the reverse.

  3. Step 3

    Loops: Tissues and Histology

    Once the chart feels comfortable, open Loops and select Tissues and Histology. Try the connective tissues too. We start connective tissue Tuesday, so it is fine to get them wrong right now. You are training your eye, not scoring points.

    Open Loops ↗

  4. Step 4

    Histology Help: practice videos

    Do not forget the practice YouTube videos on the Histology Help page. Watch one, pause on each slide, name it before the answer comes.

    Open Histology Help
Then repeat. This is not a one-time checklist. The recall steps run again every couple of days, on old material and new. If you run one full lane this week and your next score does not move, come tell Dr. Rennie exactly which steps you did, and we will adjust the method together.
