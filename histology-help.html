Histology help

Tissue slides are the part of this course students most often try to power through by staring. Staring does not work. What works is naming structures out loud, checking yourself, and coming back to the same slide a few days later.

Everything on this page is free, and every tool is grouped by the kind of help it actually gives you. Find the row that matches the trouble you are having, then pick one tool from it. You do not need all of them.

Some of these are built for a phone and some really need a bigger screen. The buttons under this section will narrow the page down to whichever one you are holding right now.

The part that actually matters

How to use any of these so it counts

You can open every link on this page and still not be ready for the practical. The tools are not the studying. This is.

  1. Draw the tissue from memory first, on paper. Before you open anything, sketch what you think the tissue looks like and label what you can. It will feel bad. That feeling is the point, because it is showing you exactly which parts you do not have yet.
  2. Now open a tool and check your drawing against it. Mark what you got wrong and what you left out entirely. The gaps you left out are the ones that cost you points, and they are invisible until you draw first.
  3. Say the identification out loud, in a full sentence. Not "stratified squamous" but what it is, where it is, and what tells you. If you cannot say it in a sentence, you recognize the picture rather than knowing the tissue, and a slightly different slide will beat you.
  4. Come back to that same tissue in two or three days. Not tomorrow, and not the night before the exam. Your recall cards will handle the timing if you let them.
  5. Bring what you are still missing to lab. Come with a specific question about a specific tissue and you will get much further than asking me to go over histology.

Before you go looking elsewhere

Do not pay for a histology app

Everything on this page is free and comes from a university that teaches this material. The app stores are full of paid histology apps with small user bases, subscriptions that are easy to miss, and slide sets smaller than what you have here for nothing.

One specific warning. Michigan pulled the Android version of SecondLook, and there are sites offering to hand you the old file directly. Do not install it. Use the web version linked above instead, which is the same content from the actual source.
