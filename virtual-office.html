Ask it here, and everybody gets the answer.

The Virtual Office is the course question board in Canvas. It is open every day of the term, it is the fastest way to get an answer, and it is the only place where your question helps somebody other than you.

Why it exists

One question, forty answers.

If you are confused about something in this course, roughly a third of the room is confused about the same thing and has not said so. When you send that question to my inbox, one person learns the answer. When you post it here, everyone does, and the next student who wonders the same thing at eleven at night finds it already answered.

That is the whole reason this board exists. It is not a formality and it is not graded. It is the fastest route to an answer.

Before you post, spend sixty seconds checking three places. Most questions are already answered in one of them, and looking first will usually be quicker than waiting for a reply.
  • Your syllabus, for anything about policy, grading, attendance or exam dates.
  • The course calendar, for what is due, when, and which pre-work belongs to which day.
  • Hootie, the owl in the bottom right corner of any course page. Ask it in plain words. It knows where things live.
Post here

Anything a classmate could also be wondering.

  • Content questions. A structure you cannot tell apart from another one. A term that appeared in two places meaning two things. A step in a pathway that does not follow.
  • Pre-work questions. A worksheet item that reads two ways. A figure that does not match the notes. Something the video covered that the sheet did not.
  • Lab questions. Which model a structure is on. What is fair game on a practical. How deep to go on a sprint list.
  • Logistics that affect everyone. Room changes, what to bring, whether a session is running.
  • Study strategy. How to use the recall cards, how to plan a week, what to do after a bad exam. Other students will answer these too, and their answers are often better than mine.
  • Answers to each other. If you know it, say so. Explaining it is how you find out whether you actually know it.
Send privately instead

Four things that are nobody else’s business.

These go to me directly, through the Canvas Inbox or srennie@solano.edu. Not because they are unwelcome, but because a public board is the wrong room for them.

How to write it

Five habits that turn a post into an answer.

  • Put the real question in the subject line. Not "question" and not "help." The subject is what a classmate scans to find out whether they already have this problem.
  • Say where you are. Week, topic, and which page, sheet or model. "Week 4 fascicle sheet, item 7" costs you six words and saves a round trip.
  • Say what you already worked out. Where your understanding stops is the useful part. It tells me what to explain and it stops me explaining what you already have.
  • One question per post. Three questions in one thread means two of them get lost. Post them separately and each gets its own answer.
  • Search the board first. Two weeks in, most of it is already there. Use the search on this discussion before you add a new one.
Worked examples

The same question, twice.

The left version is not wrong to ask. It is just slower, because the first reply has to be a question back. The right version has everything I need to answer it once.

Hard to answer

I don’t get musclesMuscles are really confusing, can someone explain?

There are thirty-four muscle competencies. Nobody can answer this without asking you which part, so the reply is a question and you have lost a day.

Ready to answer

Week 4, telling bipennate from multipennate on the modelI can sort parallel, fusiform and pennate on the worksheet, but on the arm model I cannot tell bipennate from multipennate. Is there a landmark on the tendon that separates them, or is it something you count?

Names the week, names what you can already do, and names the exact place it breaks. This gets answered in one reply, and it helps everyone who is stuck at the same step.

Hard to answer

ExamWhat’s going to be on exam 2?

The scope is published. This asks me to retype something the site already tells you, and the honest answer is a link.

Ready to answer

Exam 2 scope, does the joints sprint list count as lab practical material?The exam modules page says Exam 2 covers the skeletal block. The joints lab sprint has about twenty structures on it. Are all of those fair game on the practical, or only the ones we named in class?

You have checked the published scope and found the actual gap in it. That is a real question, and the answer is useful to the whole room.

Hard to answer

Confused about the pre-workThe pre-work makes no sense.

I cannot tell whether the sheet is broken, the video is missing, or one item is ambiguous. Three different problems, three different fixes.

Ready to answer

Cell anatomy sheet, item 7 asks for three junction types and I have fourThe notes list tight junctions, desmosomes, gap junctions and hemidesmosomes. The sheet has three blanks. Which three are you after, or is the sheet counting hemidesmosomes with desmosomes?

This one found a genuine ambiguity. Posts like this are how the materials get fixed, and I would rather hear it here than on an exam.

And one that should not be on the board at all. "I got a 62 on exam 1 and I am panicking, can we talk?" Send that one to my inbox. It is a real question and it deserves a real conversation, just not in front of forty people. I answer those first.
What to expect back

When you will hear something.

  • On this board, within twenty-four hours on a weekday. Usually much sooner, and often from a classmate before it is from me.
  • Canvas Inbox and email, forty-eight to seventy-two hours on weekdays. Which is exactly why the board is faster.
  • Your team first. For anything about a sheet or a structure, ask your team before you ask me. They are three feet away and they were in the same room.
  • Office hours run for the thirty minutes before each class, in our own room. If that never fits your schedule, message me and we will find another time.
  • Weekends are slower. A Friday-night post about a Monday deadline is a gamble. Post it Thursday.
