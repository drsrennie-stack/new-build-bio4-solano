What If My Team Is Not Participating?

Some students have asked whether they can be moved to another team because members of their current team are not communicating or participating.

I am not going to reorganize the TBL teams.

Your assigned TBL team will remain your team. Part of Team-Based Learning is learning how to work with different people, including people who communicate differently, have different strengths, or sometimes frustrate you. Moving people every time a team struggles defeats an important part of what we are practicing.

However, I also recognize an important difference between learning to work through normal team challenges and being unable to find classmates who are willing to study, communicate, and prepare with you outside of class. I am not going to let the second problem interfere with your ability to learn.

So we are going to use a hybrid approach.

Your TBL team stays your TBL team

You are still responsible for showing up, preparing, participating, communicating, and contributing to that team during our team activities.

But if you are actively looking for people to study with, review material with, prepare for lab, ask questions, or communicate about the course, I can connect you with another team that is actively doing those things.

That does not make you a member of their TBL team. Think of them as an additional study team. You can work alongside them outside of TBL while continuing to fulfill your responsibilities to your assigned team.

An important point for everyone

Access to a productive study group requires participation.

If classmates repeatedly invite you to study, ask questions, share resources, or prepare together and you choose not to engage, they are not required to stop studying because you are not participating. They are allowed to find other classmates who want to do the work.

No one is being excluded because they don't know enough anatomy. No one is being excluded because they got something wrong. No one is being excluded because they needed help.

There is a difference between struggling and disengaging.

I will happily help a student who is struggling find their way back in. But I am also going to support the students who are showing up, communicating, and actively trying to learn.

Your assigned team is not disposable when things get difficult. But neither are you limited to your assigned team when you are looking for people who are ready to study.

You have a TBL team. You can also build a learning community. I want you to do both.
Dr. Sharilyn Rennie
