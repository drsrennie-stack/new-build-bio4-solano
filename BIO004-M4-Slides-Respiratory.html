Respiratory System, Part 1

A. Nose, pharynx, larynx: the start of the airway (cadaver)

Competency 97, 98
1Hard palate
  • Def: Bony front part of the roof of the mouth
  • Contributing structures: Palatine processes (maxillae); horizontal plates (palatine bones)
  • Borders: Alveolar arches of the maxillae in front and on the sides; soft palate behind
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Greater palatine artery
  • Function: Separates oral cavity from nasal cavity; the tongue presses food against it
  • Clinical: Cleft palate when the two halves fail to fuse
3Tongue
  • Def: Muscular organ on the floor of the mouth
  • Made of: Skeletal muscle covered with mucosa
  • Parts: Extrinsic muscles (genioglossus, hyoglossus, styloglossus, palatoglossus) move it; intrinsic muscles change its shape
  • Nerve: Hypoglossal (CN XII) for all muscles except palatoglossus (vagus)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Lingual artery
  • Clinical: Hypoglossal injury: the protruded tongue deviates toward the damaged side
5Epiglottis
  • Def: Spoon-shaped flap of elastic cartilage at the top of the larynx
  • Attaches: Inside the thyroid cartilage, below the laryngeal prominence
  • Action: Tips over the laryngeal inlet during a swallow so food slides past into the esophagus
  • Nerve: Sensation by the internal laryngeal branch of the vagus
  • Clinical: Epiglottitis in a child can close the airway; never force the tongue down to look
6Vestibular folds (false vocal folds)
  • Def: Upper pair of folds inside the larynx
  • Function: Help close the airway during a swallow
  • Note: No part in making sound
7Vocal folds (true vocal cords)
  • Def: Lower pair of folds inside the larynx
  • Contributing structures: Vocal ligament from each arytenoid cartilage to the thyroid cartilage, covered by thin mucosa
  • Nerve: Recurrent laryngeal nerve moves them
  • Function: Vibrate as air passes to make sound; the gap between them is the glottis
  • Clinical: Paralysis of one fold gives hoarseness; overuse gives vocal nodules
1234567
2Soft palate
  • Def: Muscular back part of the roof of the mouth
  • Made of: Skeletal muscle covered with mucosa; ends in the uvula
  • Borders: Hard palate in front; free edge behind with the palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal arches on each side
  • Action: Lifts during a swallow to close off the nasopharynx
  • Nerve: Vagus (CN X) for all its muscles except tensor veli palatini (CN V3)
  • Clinical: Vagus injury: the uvula deviates away from the damaged side; snoring and sleep apnea from a floppy soft palate
4Esophagus
  • Def: Collapsible muscular tube from the laryngopharynx to the stomach, about 25 cm (10 inches)
  • Location: Behind the trachea, through the mediastinum and the esophageal hiatus at T10
  • Lining: Stratified squamous epithelium; adventitia outside, above the diaphragm
  • Nerve: Vagus (parasympathetic), sympathetic trunk
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Inferior thyroid, esophageal branches of the aorta, left gastric artery
  • Function: Carries the bolus to the stomach by peristalsis
  • Clinical: GERD when the lower esophageal sphincter fails; three narrowings where swallowed objects lodge

C. Larynx, anterior: cadaver beside model photo 1 of 2

Competency 99
1Thyroid cartilage
  • Def: Largest laryngeal cartilage, a shield of hyaline cartilage open at the back
  • Contributing structures: Two plates (laminae) meeting in front at the laryngeal prominence
  • Function: Protects the vocal folds; anchors the front end of the vocal ligaments
2Laryngeal prominence
  • Def: The Adam's apple; front ridge where the two plates of the thyroid cartilage meet
  • Note: Larger in males after puberty
5Trachea
  • Def: The windpipe, from the cricoid cartilage into the mediastinum
  • Location: In front of the esophagus, 10 to 12 cm long; forks at the sternal angle, T4 to T5
  • Made of: 16 to 20 C-shaped rings of hyaline cartilage, trachealis muscle, respiratory epithelium
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Inferior thyroid and bronchial arteries
  • Function: Carries air between the larynx and the primary bronchi
  • Clinical: Tracheostomy below the isthmus of the thyroid; a goiter can press on it
6Thyroid gland
  • Def: Butterfly-shaped endocrine gland
  • Location: Across the front of the trachea, just below the larynx, rings 2 to 4
  • Parts: Two lobes joined by an isthmus
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Superior thyroid (external carotid) and inferior thyroid (thyrocervical trunk) arteries
  • Clinical: Goiter can compress the trachea; the recurrent laryngeal nerves run right behind it and are at risk in surgery
123456
3Cricothyroid membrane
  • Def: Membrane between the thyroid and cricoid cartilages in front
  • Location: Just under the skin, below the vocal folds
  • Clinical: Site of an emergency airway (cricothyrotomy) when the larynx above is blocked
4Cricoid cartilage
  • Def: Signet-ring cartilage below the thyroid cartilage, narrow in front and wide behind
  • Made of: Hyaline cartilage
  • Note: The only complete ring in the airway; the trachea hangs from its lower edge; level with C6
  • Clinical: Cricoid pressure during intubation presses the esophagus shut against the spine

1Epiglottis

2Corniculate cartilage
  • Def: Small paired horns of elastic cartilage
  • Location: On the tips of the arytenoid cartilages
3Arytenoid cartilage
  • Def: Paired pyramid-shaped cartilages sitting on the back of the cricoid
  • Function: Anchor the back end of the vocal folds; pivot to open and close the glottis
  • Note: The most important paired cartilages
4Vocal folds
  • Def: Lower pair of folds inside the larynx, the true vocal cords
  • Contributing structures: Vocal ligament from each arytenoid cartilage to the thyroid cartilage
  • Nerve: Recurrent laryngeal nerve
  • Function: Vibrate as air passes to make sound
  • Clinical: Paralysis of one fold gives hoarseness
5Cricoid cartilage
  • Def: Signet-ring cartilage below the thyroid cartilage, narrow in front and wide behind
  • Made of: Hyaline cartilage
  • Note: The only complete ring in the airway; the trachea hangs from its lower edge; level with C6
  • Clinical: Cricoid pressure during intubation presses the esophagus shut against the spine
6Tracheal cartilages (C-rings)
  • Def: 16 to 20 C-shaped rings of hyaline cartilage in the tracheal wall
  • Note: The open side of each C faces back toward the esophagus
  • Function: Hold the trachea open at all times
7Trachealis muscle
  • Def: Smooth muscle bridging the open back of each C ring
  • Location: Posterior wall of the trachea, against the esophagus
  • Action: Narrows the trachea during a cough; lets the esophagus bulge forward during a swallow
  • Nerve: Vagus nerve, parasympathetic

3Inferior vena cava
  • Def: Largest vein of the lower body
