Respiratory System, Part 1

A. Nose, pharynx, larynx: the start of the airway (cadaver)

Competency 97, 98
1Hard palate
  • The bony front of the roof of the mouth, formed by the palatine processes of the maxillae and the horizontal plates of the palatine bones
  • It separates the oral and nasal cavities
3Tongue
  • Skeletal muscle covered with mucosa
  • Extrinsic muscles move it, intrinsic muscles change its shape, and the hypoglossal nerve runs almost all of them
5Epiglottis
  • The spoon-shaped elastic flap attached inside the thyroid cartilage
  • During a swallow the larynx rises and the epiglottis tips over the laryngeal inlet, so food slides past into the esophagus
6Vestibular folds (false vocal folds)
  • The upper pair of folds
  • They help close the airway during a swallow and play no part in sound
7Vocal folds (true vocal cords)
  • The lower pair of folds
  • Each holds a vocal ligament from an arytenoid to the thyroid cartilage; pearly white, they vibrate as air passes to make sound
1234567
2Soft palate
  • The muscular back of the roof of the mouth
  • It lifts with the uvula during a swallow to seal off the nasopharynx
4Esophagus
  • A collapsible muscular tube about 25 cm (10 inches) long, behind the trachea, through the mediastinum and the esophageal hiatus at T10 to the stomach
  • Lined with stratified squamous epithelium; adventitia, not serosa, above the diaphragm

1Epiglottis

2Corniculate cartilage
  • Small paired elastic cartilages sitting on top of the arytenoids
3Arytenoid cartilage
  • Paired, sitting on the back of the cricoid
  • They anchor the vocal folds and pivot to open and close the glottis, which makes them the most important paired cartilages

  • The true vocal cords, the lower pair

  • Sixteen to twenty C-shaped rings of hyaline cartilage
  • The gap at the back faces the esophagus and is closed by the trachealis muscle
7Trachealis muscle
  • Smooth muscle bridging the open back of each C ring
  • It lets the esophagus bulge forward during a swallow and narrows the trachea during a cough

Competency 97, 102
2Bronchioles
  • Airways under 1 mm with smooth muscle and no cartilage
  • With nothing to hold them open, they narrow sharply when the muscle contracts, which is the story of asthma
3Branches of pulmonary artery
  • Carry oxygen-poor blood from the right ventricle into the lung, out to the capillaries around the alveoli
4Pulmonary arterioles
  • The smallest branches of the pulmonary artery, feeding the capillary net around the alveoli
6Capillaries surrounding alveoli
  • A dense net wrapped around each alveolus, fed by the pulmonary arteries and drained by the pulmonary veins
  • Where they touch the alveolus the respiratory membrane is only three layers thick
1234567891011
1Branches of pulmonary vein
  • Carry oxygen-rich blood from the alveolar capillaries back toward the left atrium
5Pulmonary venules
  • The smallest tributaries of the pulmonary veins, draining the alveolar capillaries
7Alveolus
  • The single air sac where gas crosses into the blood
  • Its wall is mostly thin type I cells, with type II cells making surfactant and macrophages on patrol
8Opening of the alveolar duct
  • Where an alveolar duct opens into a cluster of alveoli, the alveolar sac
9Pulmonary capillaries
  • Wrap each alveolus
  • Where they press on the alveolar wall, air and blood are separated by the three-layer respiratory membrane
10Alveolar duct
  • A passage whose walls are lined entirely with alveoli
  • Part of the respiratory zone, after the respiratory bronchiole
11Visceral pleura
  • Clings to the surface of the lung and dips into its fissures
