What is a TBL, and how do I get ready?

These are the questions students ask most about TBLs, with short answers.

The basics

What is a TBL?

TBL stands for Team-Based Learning. It is a short quiz you take twice, first on your own and then with your team.

  • It checks that you came to class ready.
  • TBLs are 30% of your grade.

What happens during a TBL?

  1. iRAT (individual quiz)You answer the questions on your own.
  2. tRAT (team quiz)Your team answers the same questions together and agrees on each answer.
  3. AppealsIf your team thinks a question was unclear or the key is wrong, you can challenge it.
  4. ApplicationYour team works through problems on the same material. This part is not graded, but come ready to talk it through.

Can I use my phone or laptop?

No.

Devices stay away from the start of the iRAT to the end of the tRAT.

Are there TBLs in lab?

Not anymore.

In lab, you work on the models first and then the cadaver.

What is on a TBL?

Anything we have covered so far in the module.

The topic on the calendar tells you what the TBL focuses on, but questions can also come from earlier in the module.

When are the TBLs?

Every TBL is on your class schedule in Canvas.

Check it each week so a TBL never catches you by surprise.

New since Module 3

What is the 70% rule?

To earn your team's tRAT points on a TBL, you need at least 70% on your own iRAT first.

  • At 70% or higher, you get your iRAT score plus your team's tRAT score.
  • Below 70%, you keep your iRAT score, but you do not get the team points for that TBL.
  • This started after Exam 2 and applies to every TBL from Module 3 on.
  • The best way to stay above 70% is to do the prep before every TBL.

Why do I need 70% to get the team points?

70% is a C, the lowest passing grade in this course.

  • The tRAT works because everyone at the table brings something. Your team needs you to explain why an answer is right, catch the ones that are wrong, and help the group decide. The team points are for that, so you earn them by coming prepared.
  • It is also fair to the classmates who did the prep.
  • It keeps your grade honest. If you scored below 70% and still got the team points, your grade would say you are more ready than you are, and you would find out on the exam, where your team cannot help you. It is better to see it now, while there is still time to fix it.
  • You still take part in the tRAT and learn from the discussion. Nothing comes off your iRAT score.

Does the 70% rule change the grading in the syllabus?

No.

  • TBLs are still 30% of your grade, and each TBL is still worth 40 points: 30 from your iRAT and 10 from the tRAT.
  • The rule only decides who earns the 10 team points.
  • I announced it before it started. TBLs from Modules 1 and 2 are scored the way they always were.

Can my team score be lower than my own score?

Yes, sometimes. Your iRAT score is yours. The tRAT score is what your team decides together.

  • If you had the right answer but could not convince your team, the team can still pick the wrong one. That is part of the skill: knowing the answer is not enough, you need to be able to explain why it is right.
  • Give your reason along with your answer. "I think it is B because the tibia is the medial bone of the leg" can change minds. "I think it is B" usually will not.
  • Point to the structure, where it sits, or the competency it comes from. If your team still disagrees and you think the key is wrong, file an appeal together.
  • Over a whole term, teams that talk things through usually score higher than any one member does alone.

What if I miss a TBL?

There are no make-ups for TBLs.

Sometimes I give an unannounced TBL. There is no promise there will be one, but when there is, your lowest TBL score drops.
