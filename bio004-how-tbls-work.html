What is a TBL, and how do I get ready?

Find your question below. Each answer is short.

What is a TBL?

TBL stands for Team-Based Learning. It is a short quiz you take twice: first on your own, then with your team.

It checks that you came to class ready. TBLs are 30% of your grade.

What happens during a TBL?

  1. iRAT (individual quiz)You answer the questions on your own.
  2. tRAT (team quiz)Your team answers the same questions together and agrees on each answer.
  3. AppealsYour team can challenge a question it thinks was unclear or keyed wrong.
  4. ApplicationYour team works problems on the same material. This part is not graded, but come ready to talk it through with your team.

Can I use my phone or laptop?

No. No devices from the start of the iRAT to the end of the tRAT.

What is on a TBL?

  • Anything taught so far in the module.
  • The topic on the calendar is the focus, not the limit.

Are there TBLs in lab?

No. Lab TBLs are no longer part of the course. Lab days run on the models first, then the cadaver.

How do I get ready for a TBL?

  1. Read the notesOne section at a time.
  2. Draw it on your note sheetsFrom your notes, in one color.
  3. Watch the videosCorrect your drawings in a second color.
  4. Check the competenciesYour class schedule lists them for each day.

How much does my team affect my grade?

12.5%

Your team affects only 12.5% of your course grade: the tRAT part of each TBL plus lab quizzes. The other 87.5% comes from your own work.

New since Module 3

What is the 70% rule?

70%

To get your team's tRAT points on a TBL, you need to score at least 70% on your own iRAT first.

  • 70% or higher on your iRAT: you get your iRAT score plus your team's tRAT score.
  • Below 70% on your iRAT: you keep your iRAT score, but you do not get the team points for that TBL.

This started after Exam 2 and applies to every TBL from Module 3 on. The best way to stay above 70% is to do the prep before every TBL.

Why do I need 70% to get the team points?

The team quiz only works when everyone brings something to the table. During the tRAT, your team needs you to explain an answer, catch a wrong one, and help the group decide.

  • 70% shows you are ready to help. It is the same line as a C, the lowest passing grade in this course. Below that, you are mostly relying on your teammates for the answers.
  • Team points reward what you add to the team. They are not a free bonus. Your grade should show what you know, not only what your team knew that day.
  • It protects the students who prepared. Everyone at the table is counting on everyone else to come ready.
  • You still learn from the tRAT. If your iRAT is below 70%, you still take part in the discussion and see the answers. You just do not get the team points that day, and nothing is taken away from your iRAT score.

Can my team score be lower than my own score?

Yes, sometimes. If you had the right answer but could not convince your team, the team can still pick the wrong one. Your iRAT counts for you, but the tRAT counts what your team decides together.

That is part of the skill. In the tRAT, it is not enough to know the answer. You need to be able to explain why it is right.

  • Bring your reasons, not just your letter. "I think it is B because the tibia is the medial bone of the leg" changes minds. "I think it is B" usually does not.
  • Point to the evidence: the structure, where it sits, or the competency it comes from.
  • If your team still disagrees after you explain, and you think the key is wrong, file an appeal together.

Over a whole term, teams that talk it through usually score higher than any one member does alone.

What if I miss a TBL?

  • There are no make-ups.
  • Sometimes I give an unannounced TBL. There is no promise there will be one.
  • When there is one, your lowest TBL score drops.

When are the TBLs?

Every TBL is on your class schedule in Canvas, on the day it happens. Check it each week so a TBL never catches you by surprise.
