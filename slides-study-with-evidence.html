Study with evidence.

How to tell whether your studying is working, what to change if it is not, and how to get help that actually moves you forward.

Dr. Sharilyn Rennie

02

The main idea

Judge your preparation by what you can do on your own.

Hours spent, pages read, and videos watched are what you put in. The only way to know if it worked is to close everything and see what you can explain, draw, or point to on your own.

Does not prove it on its own

The resource is on the course site

It being there does not mean you used it.

Does not prove it on its own

You finished the assignment

Finishing it does not mean you can do it without looking.

Does not prove it on its own

You got a low score

The score says something went wrong. It does not say what. We have to look at your work to find out.

04

How class time works

I explain and demonstrate. I do not repeat the recorded lectures.

The recordings are where you meet the material for the first time. Class time is for explaining the parts people get stuck on, demonstrations, team work, and applying what you learned. None of that works if you have not done the first pass yet.

You might prefer a different format

That is a fair preference, and you can tell me about it. It does not change what you need to do: prepare, practice, and demonstrate the competencies.

You choose how you learn it. You cannot skip learning it.

Use whichever resources work for you. The competencies are the same for everyone.

05

Who does what

Two sets of responsibilities.

Mine

  • Define what you must learn.
  • Provide instruction, resources, practice, and feedback.
  • Clarify misunderstandings.
  • Pay attention to where people are getting stuck. The course structure stays the same through the term, but I can adjust a class day to spend more time on a topic that is giving a lot of people trouble, or point you to where that help is available.

Yours

  • Set aside enough study time.
  • Complete the required preparation.
  • Pick resources that fit what you need, and use them.
  • Participate in class and lab.
  • Practice on your own between classes.
  • Check whether the way you study is working.
  • When you are still stuck, ask for help and bring a specific example.

07

Start here

Time comes first.

Before you change how you study, figure out which of these two problems you have. The fixes are different.

01

Not enough time available

Work, family, other courses, and other responsibilities leave too little room for this one.

02

Hours that do not stick

You are putting in the time, but it goes to things that do not help you remember on your own.

08

Time problem 1

Not enough hours.

Better study methods make your hours count for more. They cannot make learning take no time.

Do this
  • Map your real weekly schedule, hour by hour.
  • Schedule preparation and review sessions you can actually keep.
  • Give each session one job: one competency or one set of structures.
  • Put required assignments and competency notes first.
If it still does not fit

Come talk to me early

If you keep running out of time week after week, come talk to me about your workload and what support is available before it turns into a grade problem.

09

Time problem 2

Hours that do not stick.

Rewatching, rereading, copying notes, making elaborate study guides, and organizing your materials all feel productive. They are a fine way to start. What they do not do is show you what you can remember with everything closed.

What to do instead
  1. Start with one competency.
  2. Pick a resource that covers it.
  3. Complete the competency notes.
  4. Close everything and write, say, or draw the answer.
  5. Check it and fix what you got wrong or left out.
  6. Come back to it on another day.

Reading and videos are not the problem

They are how learning starts. The step most people skip is closing them and finding out what you actually remember.

Lecture material

Three patterns and what to do about each.

See which one sounds like you. Each one has a different fix.

01

"I get it while I watch, but I cannot explain it on my own."

02

"I know the facts, but application questions get me."

03

"I know it today and cannot remember it by the exam."

11

Lecture pattern 1

"I understand it while watching, but I cannot explain it independently."

Likely issue: the material feels familiar, but you have not pulled it out of your own memory enough times yet.

Do this
  • Work on one competency at a time.
  • Complete the competency notes.
  • Close the resources.
  • Explain, write, or draw the answer from memory.
  • Check it. Is it right, and is anything missing?
  • Fix the gaps and try again.

If the whole competency is too big

Break it into smaller pieces and do one piece from memory at a time. Then put the whole thing together without looking.

12

Lecture pattern 2

"I know the facts, but I struggle with application questions."

Likely issue: you know the facts one at a time, but not how they connect or what happens when something changes.

Do this on your own
  • Explain how and why something works, not only what it is called.
  • Connect each structure to what it does.
  • Predict what changes if a structure or process changes.
  • Say why the right answer is right and why each wrong answer is wrong.
Do this in team work
  • Write down your own answer before your team talks about it.
  • Afterward, explain the reasoning again on your own.

Agreeing with a teammate's answer is not the same as being able to come up with it yourself.

13

Lecture pattern 3

"I understand it today, but I forget it before the exam."

Likely issue: you are not pulling it from memory on enough separate days, or older material is not getting reviewed.

Do this
  • Next day, test yourself before you review.
  • Spread practice across several days instead of one long block.
  • Mix older competencies in with the current ones.
  • Use short brain dumps.
  • Check your answers, fix the mistakes, and come back to those later.

Why the delay matters

Getting it right ten minutes after you study and getting it right three days later are two different things. Only the second one tells you how the exam will go.

Lab material

Three patterns and what to do about each.

01

"I recognize it with the key, but not without it."

02

"I can find it on one image, but not on another model or the cadaver."

03

"I know it in lab and cannot remember it by the next class."

15

Lab pattern 1

"I recognize it with the key, but I cannot identify it independently."

Likely issue: you are looking at the answer, or watching a teammate find it, instead of finding it yourself.

Do this
  • Run the Lab Sprint rounds.
  • Hide the key.
  • Answer before you check.
  • Practice both directions: name to structure, and structure to name.
  • Take your own turn every time.
  • Come back to the ones you missed after you have done several others.

Watching is not an attempt

When a teammate points to it first, you have seen the answer. You have not found out whether you could have found it yourself.

16

Lab pattern 2

"I identify it on one image, but not on another model or cadaver."

Likely issue: you are recognizing the picture you studied, the angle, or the spot on the page, not the structure itself.

Do this
  • Practice across every image, model, and specimen you can reach.
  • Change the viewing angle.
  • Say out loud what makes the structure look the way it does and what sits next to it.
  • Put the structures you mix up side by side and compare them.

Use lab time for what you cannot get at home

Models and specimens are only in the lab. Spend that time on them, and save atlas and Loops work for home.

17

Lab pattern 3

"I can identify it during lab, but I forget it before the next class."

Likely issue: you are not practicing it again on the days between labs.

Do this
  • Go through the set again from memory the next day.
  • Use the Lab Sprint spaced study planner.
  • Use the atlas with the answers hidden.
  • Use Loops for the structures it covers.
  • Check the full structure list. One tool may not have everything on it.
  • Mix structures from earlier labs into this week's practice.
Once you are accurate

Add time pressure

If you get them right with no time limit but fall apart when the clock is running, that is a specific, useful thing to bring to office hours.

21

Getting help

Bring your work, not just the worry.

"Bring your notes and show me what you have been doing. We will use your work to identify the next step. If you have not started, state that honestly. Your first step is to attempt the relevant preparation so we have something concrete to work with."

Look it up before you bring it

If your question has an answer you can look up, find it first in your book, the course materials, or an AI tool, and bring what you found. Office hours are for what you still cannot figure out after you have tried.

You do not need a right answer first

A half-finished attempt with the confusing part circled is exactly what I want to see. That is what we work from.

Before office hours

Fill out the preparation form and bring the work it asks for. It takes a few minutes and makes our time together count.

Office-hours preparation form

If you cannot start

If a link is broken or the directions do not make sense, contact me now instead of waiting for office hours.

22

Getting help

What office hours are good for.

Office hours used to mean "can you answer this for me?" You have the book, the recordings, the notes, and AI tools in your pocket, so do the looking-up part before you arrive. When you come in, I will usually ask you to talk me through it rather than answer it for you. What office hours are really good for is troubleshooting: your spaced recall, your study schedule, and the skills this course depends on.

Bring these
  • Help setting up spaced recall so you are testing yourself on the right days.
  • Help organizing your week, your notes, or your structure lists.
  • A drawing or explanation where you keep getting stuck at the same spot.
  • Something you learned and checked, and still could not remember the next day.
  • Two structures you keep mixing up.
  • You get it right with no time limit but not when the clock is running.
  • "I asked an AI, checked it against the book, and they disagree. Which is right?"
Do these first
  • Look the question up in the book, the notes, or the recording.
  • If you asked an AI, check its answer against the book. AI answers sound confident and are sometimes wrong.
  • Write down what you found and whether the sources agree with each other.
  • Try to explain it without looking.

If you come with a question you could have looked up and have not tried yet, we will spend five minutes on how to find it, and you will come back with the answer.

23

Your turn

The metacognition activity.

Three parts. You will look at one real piece of your own work, not just describe how hard you tried.

01

Review your current process

What you did last week, how much of it was answering without looking, and which pattern sounds like you.

02

Demonstrate your current learning

Pick one competency or a small set of structures, from the last few days or from an earlier unit. Close everything and do it from memory. Then check it and mark what was right, missing, or wrong.

03

Choose one specific change

What you will change, which resource you will use, when, and how you will know it helped. You check once today and once on another day.

24

The takeaway

Choose resources that help you learn. Then verify that you can perform on your own.

Choose→ Learn→ Retrieve→ Check→ Return

A hard week does not mean you were lazy, and a low score does not prove you did not study. The point is to find out what actually happened, look at the work, and pick one specific thing to change.
