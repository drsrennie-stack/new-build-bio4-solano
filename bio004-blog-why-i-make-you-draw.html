Why I Make You Draw

Dr. Sharilyn Rennie · Aug 24, 2026 · 2 min read

Every semester somebody asks me, usually very politely, why I make them draw.

The textbook has perfect diagrams. The atlas is beautiful. There are ten thousand labeled images online, and every one of them is better than what you are about to produce with a pencil. So why would drawing a lopsided kidney help anyone learn anatomy?

Because the textbook diagram is the artist's knowledge.

The lopsided kidney is yours.

When you look at a perfect diagram, everything on it makes sense. Of course the ureter comes off there. Of course the cortex is the outer layer. Looking at a finished picture, agreeing with it is easy. That feeling of "this all makes sense" is the same trap as rereading. Sense made by someone else is not knowledge in your head.

Drawing takes the picture away and asks you to rebuild it from nothing.

And here is what happens. Your pencil moves along just fine, and then it stops. You knew the stomach empties into the small intestine, but which part? You knew there were three layers, but what order? The pencil stops at the exact spot where your knowledge ends.

The pencil is honest. It stops precisely where you stop knowing, and no amount of nodding along can talk it out of that.

That stopping point is a gift. On a Tuesday night at your kitchen table, finding a hole costs you nothing. You open the notes, fill it, and draw it again. Finding that same hole on the lab practical costs you points. Every hole your pencil finds this week is a hole the exam cannot find later.

Drawing also forces the thing anatomy is really about: relationships. A list of structures tells you what exists. A drawing makes you commit to what is next to what, what is inside what, what comes before what. That is the understanding the exam tests, and it is the understanding you will need in a clinic, where nobody hands you a labeled diagram of the patient.

So no, it is not an art class, and nobody will ever grade your shading. Draw it ugly. Draw it from memory, check it, and draw it again. Ugly and yours beats beautiful and borrowed, every single time.

If you can draw it without looking, you know it. That is the whole test, and you can give it to yourself for free.
