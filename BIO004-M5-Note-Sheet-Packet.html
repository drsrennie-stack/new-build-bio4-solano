Module 5 · Note sheets

How this packet works

Every Module 5 competency is on one sheet with two prompts beside one open box. The number on each sheet is the same number it has in your competency packet. You build one prompt in the box. The other is there if you want more practice, and it is a good place to go the week before the exam.

Before you start

  1. Pick two pens you can tell apart. Pass one is the course notes, before any video. Pass two is a second color, after the concept video, into the same box.
  2. Stop pass one while part of the box is still empty. That empty part is the point. It is what the video fills in.
  3. Read your prompt first, then divide the box to fit it. Some prompts want one labeled drawing. Some want a table. Some want two views side by side. The division is yours to make.
  4. Words are allowed inside the drawing, as labels and inside little boxes with arrows between them. A sentence running the width of the box is not. If a box reads like a paragraph, redo it as a picture.

Why two prompts

A and B are never the same question twice. One usually asks for a labeled drawing and the other for a table or a sequence, so a student who thinks in pictures and a student who thinks in categories both get a way in. Neither is the easy option.

About the paper

You do not have to print this. Rule one box per competency onto your own paper, or set the same thing up on a tablet, and copy the prompt letter you chose at the top. A hand ruled sheet counts exactly the same as a printed one.

Lecture and lab

Each competency is tagged Lecture, Lab, or both, the same as your competency packet. Lab tagged competencies are on the lab practical, so practice those on the models and the cadaver too.

189 Sensory receptor classification

Prompt A · Labeled skin block

  • Skin block with epidermis, dermis, and hypodermis, one hair, and muscle with its tendon below
  • Free nerve endings as bare tips in the tissue; detect pain, temperature, and crude touch
  • Tactile disc in the deepest layer of the epidermis; detects light touch
  • Hair follicle receptor wrapped around the hair follicle; detects hair movement
  • Tactile corpuscle in a dermal papilla; detects fine touch
  • Lamellar corpuscle in the deep dermis or hypodermis; detects deep pressure and vibration
  • Bulbous corpuscle in the deep dermis; detects steady pressure

Prompt B · Receptor table

  • Free nerve endings: most tissues; pain, temperature, and some touch; nociceptor, thermoreceptor, or mechanoreceptor
  • Tactile discs: deepest layer of the epidermis; light touch; mechanoreceptor
  • Hair follicle receptors: around hair follicles; hair movement; mechanoreceptor
  • Tactile corpuscles: dermal papillae of hairless skin; fine touch; mechanoreceptor
  • Lamellar corpuscles: deep dermis and hypodermis; deep pressure and vibration; mechanoreceptor
  • Bulbous corpuscles: deep dermis, hypodermis, and joint capsules; steady pressure and skin stretch; mechanoreceptor
