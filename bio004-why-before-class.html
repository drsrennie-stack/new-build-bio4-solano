Why do I learn the material before class?

You see each topic on your own first, so we can spend class time on the hard part while I am there to help.

What is a flipped classroom?

In many classes you hear new material for the first time in lecture, then do the harder practice alone at home. This class flips that order.

  • You learn the new material on your own first, with your notes, the videos and your note sheets.
  • Then you come to class for the harder part: applying it and finding it on the models and the cadaver, with me there to help and your team beside you.

What is active learning?

You learn by doing something with the material, not only by listening to it.

  • TBLs, where you answer questions on your own and then work them out with your team
  • Drawing the anatomy on your note sheets
  • Finding structures on the models and the cadaver
  • Activities and review games, like Kahoot

Why does it feel harder, and why do we do it anyway?

Learning this way takes more effort than listening to a lecture, and that effort can feel like you are not learning. That feeling is normal, and the research shows it is misleading.

225
studies of college science, engineering and math courses, reviewed together
6%
higher exam scores, on average, in active learning classes
  • In that review, students in active learning classes were also much less likely to fail the course.
  • In a study of college physics students, the students in active learning classes learned more but felt like they had learned less. The extra effort felt like struggling, and they mistook it for not learning.

So when class feels hard, it usually means you are doing the work that makes the learning stick. The benefit goes to the students who take part: the ones who prep before class, speak up with their team, and try a problem before looking at the answer.

Sources: Freeman and colleagues, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2014. Deslauriers and colleagues, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2019.

What do I do before class?

  • Read the notes for the day.
  • Draw them on your note sheets.
  • Watch the concept video.
  • Read your lab structure list.

What happens in class?

  • Some days I lecture on the more difficult concepts and stop to check in with questions.
  • TBLs and problems with your team.
  • Finding structures on the models and the cadaver.

Why not just learn it in class?

The hard part of anatomy is not hearing a name for the first time. It is finding that structure on a skull with four other bones around it and no labels.

That part goes much better with me and your team in the room, so that is what we use class time for.

Will every topic be covered in lecture?

No. Not every topic needs a lecture. When there is one, it is on the more difficult concepts.

For everything else, you already have what you need to learn the material:

  • Your competencies, which list everything you can be asked
  • The concept videos and the other videos on each topic
  • Your notes, reading materials and textbook
  • Your note sheets, which walk you through each topic
  • TBLs, where you work through the material with your team

Together, these cover everything that can be on an exam. Your job is to work through these materials and learn them, then use what you learned to solve problems in class and on the exam.
