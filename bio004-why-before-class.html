How this class works · BIO 004 · Fall 2026

You learn it first. We use class time for the hard part.

In a lot of classes, you hear new material for the first time in lecture and then do the harder practice alone at home. This class flips that order, so the hardest part happens with me and your team in the room.

Flipped classroom and active learning

Learn it alone. Use it together.

01Before class, the new material.Your notes, the videos and your note sheets. You meet each topic on your own first.
02In class, the hard part.Applying it, working problems with your team, and finding structures on the models and the cadaver, with me there to help.
03Active learning, not only listening.TBLs, drawing the anatomy on your note sheets, the models and the cadaver, review games like Kahoot. You learn by doing something with the material.
How I teach this course

I teach this course in two places.

01 · Before class, on videoThe equivalent of an entire semester of lecture.I recorded it for this course. You can watch, pause and rewatch it as often as you need, which you can't do with a live lecture.
02 · In class, with meCoaching you through the hard part.
  • Live lecture on the hardest concepts, when you need it
  • Active learning assignments
  • Study success sessions
  • TBLs, where you work it out with your team
  • Lab, where you find the structures on the models and the cadaver
Most teams find their own mistakes once they talk a TBL through. When a team is still stuck, I step in and explain it. That's still teaching. It's just aimed at the part that's hardest to learn on your own.Dr. Sharilyn Rennie
Why we do it this way

It feels harder. The research says it works.

Learning this way takes more effort than listening to a lecture, and that effort can make it feel like you are not learning. That feeling is normal, and it is misleading.

01 · One large review225studies of college science, engineering and math courses, reviewed together. Students in active learning classes scored about 6% higher on exams and were much less likely to fail.
02 · One careful studyLessis how much college physics students felt they learned in active learning classes. They actually learned more. The extra effort felt like struggling, and they mistook it for not learning.
So when class feels hard, it usually means you are doing the work that makes the learning stick. The benefit goes to the students who take part: the ones who prep before class, speak up with their team, and try a problem before looking at the answer.Freeman and colleagues · PNAS 2014
Deslauriers and colleagues · PNAS 2019

Quick questions

What students ask most.

Why not just learn it in class?

The hard part of anatomy is not hearing a name for the first time. It is finding that structure on a skull with four other bones around it and no labels. That part goes much better with me and your team in the room, so that is what we use class time for.

Will every topic be covered in lecture?

No. Not every topic needs a lecture, so please do not expect every topic to get one. When there is a live lecture, it is on the more difficult concepts. For everything else, you already have what you need:

  • Your competencies, which list everything you can be asked
  • The concept videos and the other videos on each topic
  • Your notes, reading materials and textbook
  • Your note sheets, which walk you through each topic
  • TBLs, where you work through the material with your team

Together, these cover everything that can be on an exam. Your job is to work through these materials and learn them, then use what you learned to solve problems in class and on the exam.

What if the video did not make sense?
  • Use the video's chapter list to jump back to the part that confused you.
  • Bring your question to class. Working through questions like that is what class time is for.
  • Come to office hours: Mondays and Wednesdays, 10:30 am to 12:30 pm, in the conference room across from Biotech Lab 1127, or the 30 minutes before each class. Open lab is Fridays, 2:00 to 3:00 pm.
  • Post your question in the Virtual Office, where the whole class can see the answer.
