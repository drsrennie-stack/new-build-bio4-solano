What is this course about?

  • The structure of the human body, from cells to whole organ systems.
  • You study it three ways: tissues on microscope slides, models, and human donors.
  • Structure means what something is, where it is, and what it looks like.
  • When a competency asks for a basic function, you need that too. Your Competency Packet says which ones.

What materials do I need?

Required

  • Your own lab coat. It stays in the lab all term.
  • Non-latex nitrile gloves. You can split a box with your team.
  • A laptop, tablet or phone with internet, for Canvas, the concept videos and Mastery OS.
  • A way to save files as PDF, for anything you submit in Canvas.

Provided: printed module notes, pre-work and lab structure lists.

Free tools: Mastery OS, the concept videos, the Loops practice videos and the Digital Human Anatomy Atlas.

Textbook (recommended, not required)

  • OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology, 2nd edition. Free at openstax.org. This is enough for the course.
  • Marieb, Brady and Mallatt, Human Anatomy, 9th edition. Used copies are easy to find.
  • Mastering A&P is not needed for any graded work. Do not buy it.

The lab coat and gloves are the only costs. If they are a problem, talk to me or the Basic Needs Center (Building 1400, Room 1409, 707-864-7261).

What is the digital device policy?

  • No phones, smart watches, AI glasses, tablets, laptops or recording devices in the lab. Ever.
  • Silence your devices and keep them out of sight: not in a pocket, on your lap or on the table.
  • No photos, video, audio or digital notes in the lab.
  • Use digital materials outside the lab, at home or during open study hours.

Why: the people who donated their bodies cannot consent to being photographed or recorded.

Breaking this rule can mean removal from the course. You sign the digital device agreement in Canvas before your first lab.

Recording any class session without permission also breaks college policy (Board Policy 5390).

How do I act around the donors?

  • Handle every donor and specimen with care. Keep tissue covered and moist, and leave your station as you found it.
  • No jokes, nicknames, horseplay or careless language.
  • Do not talk about the donors outside the lab or on social media.
  • Working with the donors is required. Half of every lab practical is on the donors, and there is no alternative assignment.

If you feel uneasy, talk to me. Most people find the first day is the hardest. You can step outside whenever you need to.

What is the attendance policy?

  • The 20-minute rule: arriving more than 20 minutes late or leaving more than 20 minutes early counts as an absence.
  • In-class work (TBLs, exams, lab quizzes and brain dumps) cannot be made up. Missing it is a zero.
  • Every absence is treated the same. You never need to give me a reason or a doctor's note.
  • Doors lock when a lab practical starts. Late means no entry.
  • Check Canvas at least three times a week, and turn on announcement notifications.
  • Answer your team's messages within 24 hours.

Under college policy (Board Policy 5020), too many absences can get you dropped.

What if I miss something or turn it in late?

  • No late work and no make-ups.
  • For a missed TBL, see What if I miss a TBL?
  • If something serious happens in your life, come talk to me about your options in the course.

Can I use AI?

  • For studying: yes, and it is encouraged. Try Teach It to an AI in Mastery OS.
  • For graded work: only on assignments marked as AI assignments.
  • No AI on exams or on any graded part of a TBL.
  • Graded work rated above 25% AI or plagiarism gets an automatic zero.
  • Not sure if a tool counts? Ask before you submit.

The AI Honor Contract in Canvas has the full rules.

What counts as cheating?

  • Using someone else's work without credit. This includes words, ideas, images, diagrams and data, whether it is on purpose or by accident.
  • On exams: sharing information with another student, using materials that are not allowed, or looking at someone else's exam.

Cheating earns at least a zero on that work. It can mean an F in the course, and it is reported to the college. If you are not sure where the line is, ask.

How much should I study?

  • Plan on about 2 hours of study for every hour in class.
  • You are in class about 8 hours a week, so that means roughly 15 to 17 hours of study outside class.
  • Study a little every week instead of cramming. Anatomy builds on itself.
