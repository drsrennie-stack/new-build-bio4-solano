The basics

Where, what, and how to reach me.

When and where does my class meet?

In person at the Vacaville Center. Lecture and lab are on the same day, and you need to be at both.

  • Lab is always in VC 1137.
  • Your class days, times and lecture room are at the top of your syllabus in the Syllabus module.
What is this course about?

The structure of the human body, from cells all the way up to organ systems.

  • You study it three ways: tissues under the microscope, models, and human donors.
  • Knowing a structure means you can say what it is, find where it is, and recognize what it looks like.
  • Some competencies also ask for a basic function. Your Competency Packet tells you which ones.
How do I contact Dr. Rennie?

The Canvas Inbox is the best way. It keeps all of your course messages in one place.

  • You can also email me at srennie@solano.edu.
  • I reply within 48 to 72 hours on weekdays, so please do not count on a same-day answer the night before something is due.
  • If your question is about the course, post it on the Virtual Office board in Canvas. Someone else probably has the same question, and that way everyone sees the answer.
What materials do I need?
  • Your own lab coat, which stays in the lab all term
  • Non-latex nitrile gloves (you can split a box with your team)
  • A laptop, tablet or phone with internet, for Canvas, the concept videos and Mastery OS
  • A way to save files as PDFs, for anything you turn in on Canvas

I give you printed copies of the module notes, the pre-work and the lab structure lists. Mastery OS, the concept videos, the Loops practice videos and the Digital Human Anatomy Atlas are all free.

You do not need to buy a textbook. If you would like one, OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology (2nd edition) is free at openstax.org and covers everything in this course. Marieb, Brady and Mallatt's Human Anatomy (9th edition) also works, and used copies are easy to find. You do not need Mastering A&P for anything in this class, so please do not buy it.

The lab coat and gloves are your only costs. If paying for them is hard, talk to me or to the Basic Needs Center (Building 1400, Room 1409, 707-864-7261).

In the lab

The devices rule, and the donors.

What is the digital device policy?

Phones, smart watches, AI glasses, tablets, laptops and recording devices are not allowed in the lab at any time.

  • Silence your devices and put them away out of sight: not in your pocket, on your lap or on the table.
  • No photos, video, audio or digital notes in the lab. Use your digital materials outside the lab, at home or during open study hours.
  • We follow this rule because the people who donated their bodies cannot give permission to be photographed or recorded.
  • Breaking this rule can get you removed from the course. You sign the digital device agreement in Canvas before your first lab.
  • Recording any class without permission is also against college policy (Board Policy 5390).
How do I act around the donors?

Handle every donor and specimen with care.

  • Keep the tissue covered and moist, and leave your station the way you found it.
  • Please do not joke around, use nicknames or use careless language in the lab, and do not talk about the donors outside the lab or on social media.
  • Working with the donors is required. Half of the lab practical on Exams 3, 4 and 5 is on the donors, and there is no alternative assignment.
  • If you feel uneasy, come talk to me. Most people find the first day is the hardest, and you can step outside any time you need to.
Attendance and integrity

Be there, be on time, do your own work.

What is the attendance policy?
  • Arriving more than 20 minutes late or leaving more than 20 minutes early counts as an absence.
  • TBLs, exams, lab quizzes and brain dumps happen in class and cannot be made up, so missing one is a zero.
  • I treat every absence the same way. You never have to give me a reason or a doctor's note.
  • The doors lock when a lab practical starts. Once they are locked, no one can come in.
  • Check Canvas at least three times a week, and turn on notifications for announcements.
  • Answer your team's messages within 24 hours.

Under college policy (Board Policy 5020), too many absences can get you dropped from the course.

What if I miss something or turn it in late?

I do not accept late work, and there are no make-ups.

  • If you missed a TBL, read What if I miss a TBL?
  • If something serious happens in your life, please come talk to me about your options in the course.
Can I use AI?

You are welcome to use AI to study. The Teach It to an AI feature in Mastery OS is a good place to start.

  • For graded work, you can only use AI on assignments marked as AI assignments.
  • No AI on exams or on any graded part of a TBL.
  • If graded work is rated above 25% AI or plagiarism, it gets an automatic zero.
  • Not sure whether a tool counts? Ask me before you turn your work in. The AI Honor Contract in Canvas has the full rules.
What counts as cheating?
  • Plagiarism means using someone else's work without giving them credit: their words, ideas, images, diagrams and data, whether you meant to or not.
  • On exams, cheating includes sharing information with another student, using materials that are not allowed, and looking at someone else's exam.
  • Cheating earns at least a zero on that work. It can also mean an F in the course, and it is reported to the college.
  • If you are not sure where the line is, ask me.

How much should I study

Both lecture and lab add study time.

15 to 17hours a week outside classto pass with a C. These hours are on top of your time in class, which is about 8 hours a week.
25 to 26hours a week outside classfor most students who earn an A or B. You may need more if this is your first majors-level college science course. That is normal.
2:1study hours to lecture hoursis the usual guideline. In most labs you do an experiment and you are done. Anatomy lab is different: you memorize what you see for a timed practical. Make good use of your class time, especially in lab, and study a little every day instead of cramming.

Help

Accommodations, dropping, and where else to go.

How do I get accommodations?

Start with the Accessibility Services Center (ASC).

  • Building 400, Room 407, on the Fairfield campus. 707-864-7136 or asc@solano.edu.
  • Open Monday to Thursday, 8:30 am to 4:30 pm, and Friday, 8:30 am to 2:30 pm.
  • Once your accommodations are approved, send them to me through the Canvas Inbox. They start when I receive them, so an exam you have already taken cannot be retaken with accommodations.
How do I drop the course?

You drop the course yourself, through your student portal.

  • I do not do it for you, and it does not happen on its own. If you stop coming to class but never drop, you can end up with an F.
  • November 20 is the last day to drop with a W. After that, you get a letter grade.
