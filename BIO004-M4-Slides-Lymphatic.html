A. The route lymph takes (draw it) and the spleen (cadaver)

Competency 105, 107
1Right lobe of liver
  • Def: Largest lobe of the liver
  • Location: Right of the falciform ligament, under the right dome of the diaphragm, behind ribs 7 to 11
  • Blood supply: Right branches of the hepatic artery proper and hepatic portal vein
  • Note: The gallbladder sits in a hollow on its underside
125
2Spleen
  • Def: Largest lymphatic organ
  • Location: Upper left abdomen, behind the stomach, under ribs 9 to 11
  • Borders: Diaphragm above and behind; stomach in front; left kidney behind and below; splenic flexure below; the superior border is notched
  • Parts: White pulp (lymphocytes around arterioles) and red pulp (blood-filled sinuses)
  • Blood supply: Splenic artery (celiac trunk); splenic vein to the hepatic portal vein
  • Function: Fights blood-borne infection, removes old red cells, stores platelets
  • Clinical: Ruptures with a blow to the left lower ribs; a person without a spleen needs vaccines against encapsulated bacteria
5Left kidney
  • Def: Retroperitoneal organ against the posterior abdominal wall
  • Location: T12 to L3, beside the vertebral column; sits a little higher than the right kidney
  • Borders: Spleen, stomach, pancreas, and descending colon in front; diaphragm and quadratus lumborum behind
  • Blood supply: Left renal artery from the aorta; left renal vein crosses in front of the aorta

C. Primary and secondary organs photo 1 of 2

Competency 107

3Pharyngeal tonsil
  • Def: Single mass of lymphatic tissue
  • Location: Back wall of the nasopharynx
  • Function: Traps pathogens entering through the nose
  • Clinical: Enlarged adenoids block the nose and the auditory tube, so children mouth-breathe and get ear infections
5Palatine tonsil
  • Def: Paired lymphatic tissue
  • Location: Side walls of the oropharynx, between the palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal arches
  • Blood supply: Tonsillar branch of the facial artery
  • Function: Traps pathogens entering through the mouth
  • Clinical: Tonsillitis; tonsillectomy bleeding comes from the tonsillar artery
6Lingual tonsil
  • Def: Lymphatic tissue on the root (back) of the tongue
  • Location: Oropharynx, below the palatine tonsils

1Thymus
  • Def: Primary lymphatic organ
  • Location: Superior mediastinum, behind the manubrium, in front of the great vessels
  • Borders: Sternum in front; pericardium and aortic arch behind; pleura on each side
  • Blood supply: Internal thoracic and inferior thyroid arteries
  • Function: T lymphocytes mature here
  • Clinical: Large in children and shrinks after puberty; thymoma is linked to myasthenia gravis

Competency 108

Disorder: structure, what changes

Edema:tissue fluid builds up, any cause
Lymphedema:lymph vessels blocked or removed, limb swells
Lymphadenopathy:lymph nodes enlarged, infection or cancer
Lymphoma:cancer of the lymphocytes in lymphatic tissue
Splenomegaly:spleen enlarged; rupture risk in contact sports
Tonsillitis:palatine tonsils inflamed
