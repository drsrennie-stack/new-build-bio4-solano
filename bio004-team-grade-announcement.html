Your Team Is Not Controlling Your Grade

I want to clear up something about team grades, because I think there is more anxiety about this than the numbers justify.

Your team does not determine your grade in this course. Here is the actual breakdown:

Lab examsIndividual30%
Lecture examsIndividual30%
TBLMostly individual30%
Lab quizzesTeam5%
Brain dumpsIndividual5%

Even TBL is mostly individual. Each TBL is worth 40 points. 30 points come from your individual iRAT. Only 10 points come from the team.

87.5% of your grade is determined by you. When you do the math, only 12.5% of your entire course grade is determined by team performance.

And that 12.5% is designed to help you

There is a reason I use teams. Research on Team-Based Learning has found that more than 99% of teams outperform their own highest-performing individual member. In long-term TBL data, teams didn't just beat their best member. They did so by an average of about 11%.

Think about what that means. If the strongest person on a team scores an 80% individually, a well-functioning team might perform around 10 percentage points higher.

Does that mean your team will outperform you every single time? No. Sometimes you will know the right answer and your team will talk you out of it. Sometimes your team will make a bad decision. Sometimes you will underperform.

But there will also be times when someone else at that table sees something you missed, explains it to the group, and helps you get a question right that you would have missed on your own.

Don't judge the value of your team based on one question, one quiz, or one frustrating TBL. Over time, sometimes your team will cost you a point and sometimes your team will earn you points you would not have earned individually. The research tells us that those gains don't just even things out. Teams overwhelmingly come out ahead.

What about the lab quizzes?

The lab quizzes have another layer of protection built into them.

When a number is drawn, that student is responsible for answering the question. But if that student doesn't know the answer or gets it wrong, the question isn't over. Your team gets an opportunity to meet, discuss the question, and decide together whether to change the answer. If the team can determine the correct answer, you can still earn 75% of the points for that question.

That is intentional. The first answer asks, "Was the person responsible for this question prepared?" The second opportunity asks, "If one person doesn't know it, does the team collectively know it?"

If neither the individual nor the rest of the team can work out the answer, then that is useful information. At that point, the issue isn't that one teammate cost everyone points. It means the team, collectively, wasn't prepared enough on that material yet. And that's okay. Now you know what you need to go back and learn.

The goal is not perfection. The goal is preparation, contribution, and learning from one another.

Keep the numbers in perspective

87.5% of your course grade is individually determined. Only 12.5% is team-based, and even within that portion, I have deliberately built in opportunities for the team to help you recover points.

So prepare. Take your individual work seriously. Come to your team ready to contribute. Speak up when you have evidence for your answer. Listen when someone else has a good argument. Be willing to change your mind, and learn how to convince other people when you believe they are wrong.

Sometimes you will carry your team. Sometimes your team will carry you. Over the course of the semester, that's exactly how a team is supposed to work.

You do not need a perfect team. You need a team that learns how to think together.
