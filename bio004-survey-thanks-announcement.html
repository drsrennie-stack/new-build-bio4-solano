Learning with your team

Every student should have a meaningful opportunity to participate, ask questions, make mistakes, and contribute to their team’s learning.

If you understand the material well, use that knowledge to help others learn. Give teammates time to think and attempt an answer before offering yours. Ask what they notice, offer a hint, or explain your reasoning. Then give them another opportunity to try independently.

Being a supportive teammate includes:

  • Making room for others to answer before answering again.
  • Asking before stepping in with help.
  • Supporting someone’s explanation without taking over.
  • Sharing opportunities to identify structures, explain concepts, and teach.
  • Helping teammates build confidence and independence.

If you need more time or practice, that does not mean you are less capable or have less to contribute. Your questions can help the whole team uncover gaps in understanding. Everyone deserves opportunities to practice.

How preparation and class activities fit together

Although this course does not include in-person formal lectures, teaching takes place through online lectures, guided materials, demonstrations, explanations, feedback, active learning activities and our work together in class.

Your worksheets and competency lists provide a guide to the material. You can read the textbook, watch the videos, or use both to help complete the prompts. You have flexibility in how you prepare, and the quick-reference sheet can help you choose where to begin.

A clear explanation can be helpful, but understanding something while listening is only one step. We also need opportunities to see whether we can remember it, explain it, and use it ourselves. Working through the worksheet prompts and class activities gives you that practice.

There is more material than we could meaningfully address through in-person lecture alone. Preparing beforehand allows us to use our time together for practice, questions, feedback, and help with more difficult concepts.

Here is the intended rhythm:

  • Before class: Work through the assigned material using your worksheets, competency lists, textbook, and/or videos. Attempt the prompts and note questions.
  • During class: Build on that preparation through circuits, team discussion, identification, and teaching. Ask for help where you are stuck.
  • After class: Review what needs more attention, check your understanding again, and prepare for the next material.

You are not expected to arrive knowing everything. (You are expected to be minimally competent on TBLs). Preparation means making an honest attempt so we can build on it together. Discovering what you do not yet understand is a useful part of that process.

Please reach out if something is getting in the way

Working through challenging material is part of learning. Feeling excluded, dismissed, or uncomfortable asking for help is something I want to address.

Because the survey was anonymous, I cannot follow up with individual respondents. If your team experience is getting in the way of your learning, please contact me privately through Canvas or speak with me individually. You are welcome to reach out whether or not you completed the survey.

You do not need to explain your concern in front of your team or resolve it on your own first. If you are putting time into preparation but still feel unsure how to proceed, please let me know that, too.

I appreciate the effort you bring to this course, the support and kindness you bring to your teams, and the feedback that helps me improve it. Our shared goal is for everyone to become more confident and independent in remembering, explaining, and applying the material and build lifelong learning skills that will allow you to learn any material regardless of how difficult it is.
