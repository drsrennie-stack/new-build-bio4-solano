A. Nose, pharynx, larynx: the start of the airway (cadaver) · Competency 97, 98 · one card per numbered structure, same lines as the slide. Write the name, then fill each line as we go.

Name
1Hard palate
DefBony front part of the roof of the mouth
Contributing structuresPalatine processes (maxillae); horizontal plates (palatine bones)
BordersAlveolar arches of the maxillae in front and on the sides; soft palate behind
Blood supply (info only)Greater palatine artery
FunctionSeparates oral cavity from nasal cavity; the tongue presses food against it
ClinicalCleft palate when the two halves fail to fuse
2Soft palate
DefMuscular back part of the roof of the mouth
Made ofSkeletal muscle covered with mucosa; ends in the uvula
BordersHard palate in front; free edge behind with the palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal arches on each side
ActionLifts during a swallow to close off the nasopharynx
NerveVagus (CN X) for all its muscles except tensor veli palatini (CN V3)
ClinicalVagus injury: the uvula deviates away from the damaged side; snoring and sleep apnea from a floppy soft palate
3Tongue
DefMuscular organ on the floor of the mouth
Made ofSkeletal muscle covered with mucosa
PartsExtrinsic muscles (genioglossus, hyoglossus, styloglossus, palatoglossus) move it; intrinsic muscles change its shape
NerveHypoglossal (CN XII) for all muscles except palatoglossus (vagus)
Blood supply (info only)Lingual artery
ClinicalHypoglossal injury: the protruded tongue deviates toward the damaged side
4Esophagus
DefCollapsible muscular tube from the laryngopharynx to the stomach, about 25 cm (10 inches)
LocationBehind the trachea, through the mediastinum and the esophageal hiatus at T10
LiningStratified squamous epithelium; adventitia outside, above the diaphragm
NerveVagus (parasympathetic), sympathetic trunk
Blood supply (info only)Inferior thyroid, esophageal branches of the aorta, left gastric artery
FunctionCarries the bolus to the stomach by peristalsis
ClinicalGERD when the lower esophageal sphincter fails; three narrowings where swallowed objects lodge
5Epiglottis
DefSpoon-shaped flap of elastic cartilage at the top of the larynx
AttachesInside the thyroid cartilage, below the laryngeal prominence
ActionTips over the laryngeal inlet during a swallow so food slides past into the esophagus
NerveSensation by the internal laryngeal branch of the vagus
ClinicalEpiglottitis in a child can close the airway; never force the tongue down to look
6Vestibular folds (false vocal folds)
DefUpper pair of folds inside the larynx
FunctionHelp close the airway during a swallow
NoteNo part in making sound
7Vocal folds (true vocal cords)
DefLower pair of folds inside the larynx
Contributing structuresVocal ligament from each arytenoid cartilage to the thyroid cartilage, covered by thin mucosa
NerveRecurrent laryngeal nerve moves them
FunctionVibrate as air passes to make sound; the gap between them is the glottis
ClinicalParalysis of one fold gives hoarseness; overuse gives vocal nodules
