Round 1. Find the deciding feature, then say why the others fail

Every option is a true statement. Only one of them decides the pair, and a pair is not finished until you have also said what is wrong with the other three. Knowing the right answer is worth less than knowing why the near-misses are near-misses, because on the practical the near-miss is the thing you will actually be looking at.

Round 2. Mixed field run

Fields come at you mixed on purpose, never two of the same kind in a row. It feels worse than studying one category at a time. It is also the only version that matches what a practical actually does to you. Name the tissue, then name the feature that decided it, then find out.

When you miss one

A miss is not one thing. Tag it, because the three kinds need three different fixes and only one of them is fixed by your key.

  • I never saw the featureA looking problem. Go back to the scope, find that feature on a slide you have already named correctly, and make yourself see it before you move on.
  • I saw it and named the feature wrongA vocabulary problem. This is the one your recall cards fix, tonight, not in the room.
  • I named the feature right and still picked the wrong tissueA key problem. Your deciding feature was not actually deciding. Come back to Round 1 and rewrite that line.
