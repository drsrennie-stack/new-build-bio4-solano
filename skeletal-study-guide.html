1. Cartilage

Supporting tissue
  1. What is articular cartilage? What is it made of? What is its function? Recall
  2. Name the three types of cartilage and give a location for each. Recall Added
  3. Name the two ways cartilage grows, and say where each one starts. Apply Added
  4. Why is cartilage hard to heal? Reason

2. The skeletal system and bone functions

Overview
  1. Name the different components of the skeletal system and give a brief description of each. Include any distinguishing features. Recall
  2. What is osteology? Recall
  3. Name the basic functions of bone and describe each one. Apply

3. Gross anatomy of a long bone

Macroscopic
  1. What are the major parts of a long bone at the macroscopic level? What blood vessels supply or drain each? Recall
  2. Describe the diaphysis. What structures are found in and around it, and what are their functions? Recall
  3. What is the periosteum? What is it composed of, naming the two layers and their components? What are its functions? Describe its blood supply. Apply
  4. What are Sharpey's fibers? Recall
  5. Where is the metaphysis, and what does it hold? Recall Added
  6. What is the medullary cavity, and what lines the internal bone surfaces? Recall Added
  7. What passes through the nutrient foramen, and why does a long bone need one? Apply Added
  8. Which structure of the bone is innervated or rich in nerves? Reason

4. Bone matrix and mineralization

Chemistry
  1. What is the most abundant mineral salt in the extracellular matrix of bone? Recall
  2. What are the two main mineral salts that combine to create bone matrix? Recall
  3. What forms hydroxyapatite crystals? What other minerals do the mineral salts combine with to lead to calcification? Apply
  4. It is the combination of ______ and ______ that gives bone its characteristics. Hardness of bone is due to ______. Flexibility of bone depends on ______, which gives it tensile strength. Apply
  5. What is tensile strength? Reason

5. Bone microanatomy and bone cells

Histology
  1. Name the four different types of bone cells. Make a mind map that helps you distinguish each. Know what they are, where they are found, how they function, and which ones are mitotically active. Apply
  2. What are the differences between compact bone and spongy bone? Define in terms of location, characteristics and function. Apply
  3. Be able to define and identify all of the component histological structures: concentric, interstitial and circumferential lamellae, central (Haversian) canal, lacunae, canaliculi, osteocytes, Volkmann's canals (perforating fibers). Recall
  4. What is the basic unit of bone? Describe it. Recall
  5. Osteons align with the lines of stress, so a bone is strongest along its long axis. Predict which kind of force is most likely to break it. Reason Added

6. Bone growth, ossification and the growth plate

Development
  1. Name the two routes of ossification. What does each one build, and what does each start from? Recall Added
  2. Name the two ways a long bone grows. Give the direction of each and where it happens. Apply Added
  3. Name the five zones of the growth plate in order, from the epiphysis down to the diaphysis, and say what happens in each. Recall Added
  4. Differentiate between the epiphyseal plate and the epiphyseal line. Recall
  5. Why is damage to the cartilage or epiphyseal plate concerning? How does it do this? Reason
  6. The growth plate is cartilage, so on a child's X-ray it looks like a gap. Explain why a clinician describes a pediatric fracture by how it crosses the plate. Reason Added
  7. An X-ray shows the epiphyseal line rather than a plate. What does that tell you about the skeleton? Reason Added

7. Marrow, resorption, fractures and repair

Clinical
  1. Differentiate between the different types of bone marrow. Recall
  2. What is a bone marrow biopsy? Where is it normally performed, and why? What are they looking at and what information are they drawing from it? Is it painful? Apply
  3. What is bone resorption? Recall
  4. What is a fracture of the bone? Distinguish between the different fractures and treatments. What is the goal of fracture repair? Differentiate between open and closed reduction. Apply

Vocabulary

  2. Differentiate between bones that are axial skeleton and bones that are appendicular skeleton. Recall
  3. Know the different bone surface markings. This often comes up as multiple choice or matching. Recall

Axial

  2. Know the major bones of the skull and any distinguishing features on each: mandible, temporal, parietal, sphenoid, ethmoid and the rest. Recall

  4. What is the glabella? What is the metopic suture? Recall
  5. What are the components of the TMJ? Recall

  7. What is the cribriform plate? What is the crista galli? What attaches to it? Recall
  8. Name the structures that make up the nasal septum. Recall
  9. What is the name of the condition when an ear infection spreads to the bone behind the ear? What is the risk? Reason
