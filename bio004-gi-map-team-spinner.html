BIO 004 · Human Anatomy · Team project

Build and teach your section of the GI tract

Your team owns one section of the digestive system. Make a map of it on paper, then teach it to the class. You have today to build and Thursday to finish and teach.

Your map must show
  • Anatomy. Every structure in your section, drawn and labeled.
  • Wall. The layers of the wall in your section, inside to outside, and what is different about them here.
  • Cells and what they make. Each cell type, where it sits, and the substance it produces.
  • Nerves. The two enteric plexuses, the parasympathetic nerve, and the sympathetic ganglion that reach your section.
  • Blood supply. The main artery that feeds your section.
  • Basic function. One or two sentences only. This is an anatomy course, so keep the mechanism out and the structure in.
  • Two memory tools. Teach with at least two mnemonics or analogies. Your helper sheet gives you two; use those or invent better ones.
How it works
  • Today: plan and build your map. Divide the work so every person owns part of it.
  • Thursday: finish, then teach your section to the class using your map.
  • Allowed: the course notes, your own handwritten notes, your textbook, the models, the Digital Atlas, and your one-page helper sheet.
  • Teaching: every team member speaks. Point to the structure, name it, and say what it is made of or what it makes.
  • Take a picture of this prompt and of your map so the whole team has it.
The five sections
How your map and teaching are scored
  • Structures: everything on your section list is drawn and labeled correctly.
  • Wall: the layers match your section, and you can say what is different here.
  • Cells: each cell is placed where it lives and matched to what it makes.
  • Nerves and vessels: both plexuses, the parasympathetic nerve, the sympathetic ganglion, and the main artery are named.
  • Teaching: every member taught, used the map, and asked the class to point and name.
  • Memory tools: at least two mnemonics or analogies were taught, and they were accurate.
