A. Anterior thigh and the femoral triangle (cadaver) photo 2 of 2

Competency 127, lab
2Femoral vein
  • The deep vein of the thigh, on the medial side of the artery in the femoral triangle
  • Receives the great saphenous vein, then becomes the external iliac vein at the inguinal ligament
3Femoral artery
  • The external iliac artery once it passes under the inguinal ligament
  • Runs through the femoral triangle, gives off the deep femoral artery, then passes the adductor hiatus to become the popliteal artery
123
1Femoral nerve
  • The largest branch of the lumbar plexus (L2 to L4)
  • Enters the thigh under the inguinal ligament lateral to the femoral artery and supplies the anterior thigh: quadriceps and sartorius

B. Gluteal region and posterior thigh (cadaver) photo 2 of 2

Competency 127, lab
2Gluteus minimus
  • The deepest gluteal muscle, under gluteus medius
  • Abducts the hip and steadies the pelvis when the opposite foot is off the ground
123
1Piriformis
  • A deep gluteal muscle from the sacrum to the greater trochanter
  • The sciatic nerve usually leaves the pelvis just below it
3Sciatic nerve
  • From the sacral plexus (L4 to S3), the largest nerve in the body
  • Leaves through the greater sciatic foramen below piriformis, runs deep to gluteus maximus and the hamstrings, and splits above the knee into the tibial and common fibular nerves

  • The most medial anterior leg muscle

  • Supplies fibularis longus and brevis, the lateral leg muscles that evert the foot

  • The flat calf muscle under gastrocnemius
  • Plantar flexes the foot through the calcaneal tendon; works in standing and walking
6Fibularis brevis
  • A lateral leg muscle under fibularis longus
  • Everts the foot; supplied by the superficial fibular nerve
