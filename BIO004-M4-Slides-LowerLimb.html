Lower Limb: Muscles, Nerves and Vessels

A. Anterior thigh and the femoral triangle (cadaver) photo 1 of 2

Competency 127, lab
1Iliopsoas
  • Def: Psoas major and iliacus acting as one muscle, the main hip flexor
  • Origin: Psoas major: bodies and transverse processes of T12 to L5. Iliacus: iliac fossa
  • Insertion: Lesser trochanter of the femur
  • Action: Flexes the hip; psoas also flexes the trunk
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Psoas: L1 to L3 ventral rami. Iliacus: femoral nerve
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Lumbar arteries, iliolumbar artery, medial femoral circumflex
  • Clinical: Psoas sign: pain on passive hip extension with an inflamed appendix lying on the muscle
2Vastus intermedius
  • Def: Deep head of the quadriceps, under rectus femoris
  • Origin: Anterior and lateral shaft of the femur
  • Insertion: Patella and tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament
  • Action: Extends the knee
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Femoral nerve (L2 to L4)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Lateral femoral circumflex artery
4Vastus medialis
  • Def: Medial head of the quadriceps, the teardrop above the knee
  • Origin: Intertrochanteric line and medial lip of the linea aspera
  • Insertion: Patella and tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament
  • Action: Extends the knee; its lowest fibers hold the patella in its groove
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Femoral nerve (L2 to L4)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Femoral artery branches
  • Clinical: Weak vastus medialis lets the patella track laterally (patellofemoral pain)
12345
3Vastus lateralis
  • Def: Lateral head of the quadriceps, the largest
  • Origin: Greater trochanter and lateral lip of the linea aspera
  • Insertion: Patella and tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament
  • Action: Extends the knee
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Femoral nerve (L2 to L4)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Lateral femoral circumflex artery
  • Clinical: Common intramuscular injection site in infants
5Rectus femoris
  • Def: Quadriceps head that crosses both the hip and the knee
  • Origin: Anterior inferior iliac spine
  • Insertion: Patella, then the tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament
  • Action: Extends the knee, flexes the hip
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Femoral nerve (L2 to L4)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Lateral femoral circumflex artery
  • Clinical: Strained in sprinters and kickers; the only quadriceps head that can be torn at the hip

A. Anterior thigh and the femoral triangle (cadaver) photo 2 of 2

Competency 127, lab
2Femoral vein
  • Def: Deep vein of the thigh
  • Location: Medial to the femoral artery in the femoral triangle
  • Receives: Great saphenous vein, deep femoral vein
  • Course: Becomes the external iliac vein above the inguinal ligament
  • Clinical: Site for central venous access; a clot here is a DVT that can reach the lungs
3Femoral artery
  • Def: Main artery of the thigh
  • Origin: The external iliac artery once it passes under the inguinal ligament
  • Course: Through the femoral triangle between the nerve and the vein; gives the deep femoral artery; passes the adductor hiatus to become the popliteal artery
  • Supplies: Thigh, through its deep femoral branch
  • Borders: Femoral triangle: inguinal ligament above, sartorius lateral, adductor longus medial
  • Clinical: Femoral pulse below the inguinal ligament; the usual entry point for catheters
123
1Femoral nerve
  • Def: Largest branch of the lumbar plexus
  • Origin: L2 to L4
  • Location: Enters the thigh under the inguinal ligament, lateral to the femoral artery (NAVEL, lateral to medial)
  • Supplies: Quadriceps, sartorius, pectineus, iliacus; skin of the anterior thigh and, by the saphenous nerve, the medial leg
  • Clinical: Injury weakens knee extension and loses the patellar reflex

B. Gluteal region and posterior thigh (cadaver) photo 1 of 2

Competency 127, lab
2Biceps femoris
  • Def: Lateral hamstring with two heads
  • Origin: Long head: ischial tuberosity. Short head: linea aspera
  • Insertion: Head of the fibula
  • Action: Flexes the knee; long head extends the hip
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Long head: tibial division of the sciatic. Short head: common fibular division
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Perforating branches of the deep femoral artery
  • Borders: Its tendon is the lateral edge of the popliteal fossa
  • Clinical: Hamstring strain at the ischial tuberosity in sprinters; its tendon is the landmark for the common fibular nerve at the knee
123
1Semimembranosus
  • Def: Deep medial hamstring with a flat membranous tendon
  • Origin: Ischial tuberosity
  • Insertion: Medial condyle of the tibia
  • Action: Flexes the knee, extends the hip, medially rotates the flexed leg
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Tibial division of the sciatic nerve (L5 to S2)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Perforating branches of the deep femoral artery
  • Borders: Medial edge of the popliteal fossa
3Semitendinosus
  • Def: Superficial medial hamstring with a long cord-like tendon
  • Origin: Ischial tuberosity
  • Insertion: Medial surface of the upper tibia (pes anserinus)
  • Action: Flexes the knee, extends the hip, medially rotates the flexed leg
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Tibial division of the sciatic nerve (L5 to S2)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Perforating branches of the deep femoral artery
  • Borders: With semimembranosus, the medial edge of the popliteal fossa
  • Clinical: Its tendon is harvested for ACL reconstruction

B. Gluteal region and posterior thigh (cadaver) photo 2 of 2

Competency 127, lab
2Gluteus minimus
  • Def: Deepest of the three gluteal muscles
  • Origin: Outer surface of the ilium between the anterior and inferior gluteal lines
  • Insertion: Greater trochanter, anterior surface
  • Action: Abducts and medially rotates the hip; keeps the pelvis level when the opposite foot is off the ground
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Superior gluteal nerve (L4 to S1)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Superior gluteal artery
  • Clinical: Weak gluteus medius and minimus give the Trendelenburg sign: pelvis drops on the unsupported side
123
1Piriformis
  • Def: Deep gluteal muscle and the landmark of the gluteal region
  • Origin: Anterior surface of the sacrum
  • Insertion: Greater trochanter, upper border
  • Action: Laterally rotates the extended hip, abducts the flexed hip
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Nerve to piriformis (S1, S2)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Superior and inferior gluteal arteries
  • Borders: Superior gluteal vessels and nerve leave above it; sciatic nerve and inferior gluteal vessels leave below it
  • Clinical: Piriformis syndrome: the muscle compresses the sciatic nerve, giving buttock pain down the leg
3Sciatic nerve
  • Def: Largest nerve in the body
  • Origin: Sacral plexus, L4 to S3
  • Course: Leaves through the greater sciatic foramen below piriformis, runs deep to gluteus maximus and the hamstrings, splits above the knee
  • Branches: Tibial nerve and common fibular nerve
  • Supplies: Hamstrings, then the whole leg and foot through its branches
  • Clinical: Sciatica from a herniated disc; an injection in the upper outer quadrant of the buttock avoids it

C. Anterior and lateral leg (cadaver) photo 1 of 2

Competency 127, lab
1Tibialis anterior
  • Def: Most medial muscle of the anterior leg
  • Origin: Lateral condyle and upper lateral tibia, interosseous membrane
  • Insertion: Medial cuneiform and base of the first metatarsal
  • Action: Dorsiflexes and inverts the foot
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Deep fibular nerve (L4, L5)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Anterior tibial artery
  • Clinical: Shin splints; anterior compartment syndrome; paralysis gives foot drop
3Superficial fibular nerve
  • Def: Superficial branch of the common fibular nerve
  • Course: Lateral compartment, then pierces the fascia to the top of the foot
  • Supplies: Fibularis longus and brevis; skin of the lower lateral leg and top of the foot
  • Clinical: Injury loses eversion; the foot tends to invert
5Extensor digitorum longus
  • Def: Anterior leg muscle lateral to tibialis anterior
  • Origin: Lateral condyle of the tibia, upper fibula, interosseous membrane
  • Insertion: Middle and distal phalanges of toes 2 to 5
  • Action: Extends the toes, dorsiflexes the foot
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Deep fibular nerve (L5, S1)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Anterior tibial artery
123456
2Fibularis longus
  • Def: Superficial muscle of the lateral leg
  • Origin: Head and upper two thirds of the fibula
  • Insertion: Medial cuneiform and base of the first metatarsal, after crossing under the sole
  • Action: Everts and plantar flexes the foot; supports the arches
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Superficial fibular nerve (L5, S1)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Fibular artery
  • Clinical: Weak evertors mean repeated inversion ankle sprains
4Soleus
  • Def: Flat calf muscle under gastrocnemius
  • Origin: Soleal line of the tibia, upper fibula
  • Insertion: Calcaneus by the calcaneal tendon
  • Action: Plantar flexes the foot; holds you upright in standing and walking
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Tibial nerve (S1, S2)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Posterior tibial and fibular arteries
  • Clinical: Deep veins inside it are a common site for DVT in a bedridden patient
6Fibularis brevis
  • Def: Lateral leg muscle under fibularis longus
  • Origin: Lower two thirds of the fibula
  • Insertion: Base of the fifth metatarsal
  • Action: Everts the foot
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Superficial fibular nerve (L5, S1)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Fibular artery
  • Clinical: A hard inversion can pull its tendon off the fifth metatarsal (avulsion fracture)

C. Anterior and lateral leg (cadaver) photo 2 of 2

Competency 127, lab
1Anterior tibial artery
  • Def: One of the two end branches of the popliteal artery
  • Course: Passes forward through the interosseous membrane, down the anterior leg with the deep fibular nerve, onto the foot as the dorsalis pedis artery
  • Supplies: Anterior compartment of the leg
  • Clinical: Dorsalis pedis pulse on top of the foot; lost in peripheral arterial disease
3Deep fibular nerve
  • Def: Deep branch of the common fibular nerve
  • Course: Runs with the anterior tibial artery in the anterior compartment
  • Supplies: Tibialis anterior, extensor digitorum longus, extensor hallucis longus, fibularis tertius; skin between toes 1 and 2
  • Clinical: Damage gives foot drop and a high-stepping gait

D. Posterior leg, and the veins (cadaver) photo 1 of 2

Competency 127, lab
2Soleus
  • Def: Flat calf muscle under gastrocnemius
  • Origin: Soleal line of the tibia, upper fibula
  • Insertion: Calcaneus by the calcaneal tendon
  • Action: Plantar flexes the foot; holds you upright in standing and walking
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Tibial nerve (S1, S2)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Posterior tibial and fibular arteries
  • Clinical: Deep veins inside it are a common site for DVT in a bedridden patient
123
1Gastrocnemius
  • Def: Superficial calf muscle with two heads
  • Origin: Medial and lateral condyles of the femur
  • Insertion: Calcaneus by the calcaneal tendon
  • Action: Plantar flexes the foot, flexes the knee
  • Nerve info only, not on the exam
    Tibial nerve (S1, S2)
  • Blood supply info only, not on the exam
    Sural branches of the popliteal artery
  • Borders: Its two heads form the lower edges of the popliteal fossa
  • Clinical: Tennis leg: tear of the medial head; calf pump failure contributes to DVT
3Calcaneal tendon
  • Def: The Achilles tendon, thickest tendon in the body
  • Contributing structures: Gastrocnemius and soleus share it
  • Insertion: Posterior calcaneus
  • Function: Transfers the pull of both muscles to plantar flex the foot
  • Clinical: Rupture gives a sudden pop and a positive Thompson test (squeeze the calf, no plantar flexion)

Competency 127, lab
2Tibial nerve
  • Def: Larger branch of the sciatic nerve
  • Course: Through the popliteal fossa, down the back of the leg beside the posterior tibial artery, behind the medial malleolus, into the sole

  • Course: Down the back of the leg deep to the calf muscles, beside the tibial nerve, behind the medial malleolus
  • Supplies: Posterior compartment of the leg and, through the plantar arteries, the sole
  • Clinical: Pulse felt behind the medial malleolus

Map. Arteries of the lower limb

Competency 127
1External iliac artery
  • Runs along the pelvic brim; renamed at the inguinal ligament
2Femoral artery
  • Femoral triangle, between the nerve (lateral) and vein (medial). Pulse below the inguinal ligament
3Deep femoral artery
  • Main supply of the thigh; gives the circumflex femoral and perforating arteries
4Medial and lateral circumflex femoral
  • Around the neck of the femur; supply the head of the femur
5Perforating arteries
  • Through adductor magnus to the hamstrings
6Popliteal artery
  • Behind the knee, deep in the popliteal fossa. Pulse with the knee bent
7Anterior tibial artery
  • Passes forward through the interosseous membrane; anterior leg with the deep fibular nerve
8Dorsalis pedis artery
  • Top of the foot, lateral to the extensor hallucis longus tendon. Pulse
9Posterior tibial artery
  • Deep to the calf muscles with the tibial nerve. Pulse behind the medial malleolus
10Fibular artery
  • Lateral side of the posterior compartment, beside the fibula
11Medial and lateral plantar arteries
  • Into the sole, behind the medial malleolus

Map. Veins of the lower limb

Competency 127
1External iliac vein
  • The femoral vein with a new name above the inguinal ligament
2Femoral vein
  • Medial to the femoral artery in the triangle; receives the great saphenous at the saphenous opening
3Deep femoral vein
  • Runs with the deep femoral artery
4Popliteal vein
  • Behind the knee; receives the small saphenous vein
5Anterior tibial veins
  • Paired deep veins with the artery
6Posterior tibial veins
  • Paired deep veins with the artery; drain the plantar veins
7Fibular veins
  • With the fibular artery
8Great saphenous vein
  • Longest vein in the body. Starts at the medial end of the dorsal venous arch, passes IN FRONT of the medial malleolus, up the medial leg and thigh, into the femoral vein
9Small saphenous vein
  • Lateral end of the dorsal venous arch, BEHIND the lateral malleolus, up the middle of the calf, into the popliteal vein
10Dorsal venous arch
  • On the top of the foot; the two saphenous veins start from its ends

Deep veins run with the arteries and share their names (dark blue). Superficial veins (light blue) run in the skin and drain into them. · dashed lines are the landmarks: use them to tell where you are. In Present, each click adds the next segment; click its box for the note.

Map. Nerves of the lower limb

Competency 127
1Lumbar plexus (L1 to L4)
  • In psoas major, on the posterior abdominal wall
2Femoral nerve (L2 to L4)
  • Under the inguinal ligament, lateral to the femoral artery. Anterior thigh: quadriceps, sartorius
3Saphenous nerve
  • Skin of the medial leg and foot; runs with the great saphenous vein. The only femoral branch below the knee
4Obturator nerve (L2 to L4)
  • Through the obturator foramen. Medial thigh: adductors; skin of the medial thigh
5Sacral plexus (L4 to S4)
  • On piriformis, in the pelvis
6Sciatic nerve (L4 to S3)
  • Largest nerve. Out through the greater sciatic foramen below piriformis, deep to gluteus maximus, down the posterior thigh under the hamstrings
7Superior and inferior gluteal nerves
  • Superior: gluteus medius, minimus. Inferior: gluteus maximus
8Tibial nerve (L4 to S3)
  • Straight down through the popliteal fossa and the posterior leg with the posterior tibial artery, behind the medial malleolus
9Medial and lateral plantar nerves
  • Into the sole through the tarsal tunnel
10Common fibular nerve (L4 to S2)
  • Along the biceps femoris tendon to the lateral knee, then around the neck of the fibula. Injury here gives foot drop
11Superficial fibular nerve
  • Lateral leg: fibularis longus and brevis (eversion); skin of the top of the foot
12Deep fibular nerve
  • Anterior leg with the anterior tibial artery: dorsiflexors; skin between toes 1 and 2
