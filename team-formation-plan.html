BIO 004 · Faculty planning · Fall 2026

Team Formation Plan.

Everything for building TBL teams in one place: the two-minute method for day one, the automated method for whenever the survey data exists, the exact survey to build, and the fallback that needs no technology at all. Teams are 5 to 7, instructor-formed, permanent for the term, and announced with a sentence about how they were made.

The principle

Balance the one thing students notice. Randomize the rest.

Careful multi-factor balancing and plain random assignment produce teams that perform about the same. What genuinely hurts is friend groups picking themselves, and one team collecting all the clinical experience while another has none. So the working rule is: split by hands-on healthcare experience, shuffle each group, deal them evenly, and stop there unless survey data is already in hand. Then tell students the teams were assigned deliberately and are permanent. The sentence matters as much as the method.

Method A · Day one, two minutes

Quick Teams

Open Quick Teams from the faculty hub. Paste the roster from your class list, one name per line, with an asterisk after anyone with hands-on healthcare experience. Set the team size, deal, and put it on the projector. Names never leave the browser.

Getting the asterisks: at the door or on the sign-in sheet, ask everyone with hands-on patient care experience to mark it. Or ask for a show of hands during the welcome slide and mark them off the roster. Then run the brain dump clock for two minutes while you paste and deal.

Announce it: "I assigned these teams so every team starts with a similar mix of experience. You are with these people all term. Find your team now." Then straight into the charter rounds.

Method B · Automated, whenever survey data exists

The full generator

Team Generator takes a Google Form CSV and snake-deals by background strength, then swaps until every team has an organizer and a tech-comfortable member, shares a meeting window, evens out work hours, and leaves nobody isolated. It also prints a transparency report you can show the class. Use it when you can survey ahead of time, or run the survey in the first ten minutes of class from a QR on screen, download the CSV from the linked Sheet, and generate while students watch the tools slides.

Build the Google Form with these questions, in this order, so the generator's column mapping lines up:

  1. Your full name Short answer
  2. Highest degree you have completed High school diploma or currently completing prerequisites · Associate degree or some college credit · Bachelor's degree or higher in a non-STEM field · Bachelor's degree or higher in a STEM field
  3. Healthcare training or licensure No healthcare experience · Entry-level patient care (CNA, PCT, MA, EMT-B) · Licensed allied health professional (RN, RT, paramedic, rad tech)
  4. On a team, I prefer to... Lead and organize the group · Help plan and keep us organized but not necessarily lead · Contribute and follow the group's plan
  5. How comfortable are you with technology and learning new apps? Very comfortable, I enjoy tech · Comfortable · Neutral · Not very comfortable
  6. Which sounds more like you? More extroverted · Somewhere in between · More introverted
  7. When are you generally free to meet outside of class? (check all that apply) Weekday mornings · Weekday afternoons · Weekday evenings · Weekends
  8. About how many hours per week do you work for pay? I don't work for pay right now · Under 10 hours · 10 to 20 hours · 20 to 30 hours · More than 30 hours
  9. Gender (optional) and Is English an additional language for you? (optional) Used only to avoid leaving anyone as the lone member of a group
Method C · No technology at all

The transparent line-up

Everyone stands in one line across the room, ordered by hands-on healthcare experience, none at one end and most at the other. Then each stretch of the line re-sorts by prior anatomy or biology coursework. Count off by the number of teams: all the ones are Team 1, all the twos are Team 2, and so on. Less balanced on the soft factors than the generator, but fair, fast, and students watch it happen, which is its own kind of credibility.

After the teams exist

Lock them in

Run the charter rounds the same day, photograph every signed page, and enter the rosters in Canvas groups that evening. Point students to Your TBL Team for how the three layers of peer learning work. If a team loses members to drops in the first two weeks and falls below four, fold it into neighbors rather than reshuffling everyone; the charter and the permanence are worth more than a perfectly even count.
