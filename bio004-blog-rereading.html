Rereading Feels Like Learning. It Isn't.

Dr. Sharilyn Rennie · Aug 24, 2026 · 2 min read

Here is the most common way students study for this class.

Open the notes. Read them again. Highlight some things. Watch the lecture video a second time. Look at the slides one more time. Close everything and feel pretty good.

It feels good because the material starts to look familiar. Second time through, the page is recognizable. Nothing surprises you. Your brain reads that smoothness as knowing.

It isn't knowing.

It is recognizing. And the exam never asks whether the page looks familiar.

The exam puts a slide or a structure in front of you and asks you to produce the answer out of your own head, with the notes closed. That is a completely different skill from recognizing an answer when it is in front of you. You can be excellent at one and terrible at the other, and rereading only ever trains the first.

This is why a student can honestly say "but I studied for hours" and mean it, and still get a score that doesn't match. The hours were real. They were spent on the wrong skill.

Train the skill the exam actually tests

Retrieval. Closed notes, blank page, you producing the material from memory.

Do a brain dump: blank page, write everything you know about a topic, then open the notes and see what you missed. Flip the tissue chart to Recall and answer before you reveal. Run your recall cards. Draw the structure from memory. Explain a concept out loud to a teammate, or to your dog, and notice exactly where you stall.

Fair warning: this feels worse than rereading. Retrieval is uncomfortable. You reach for an answer and it isn't there, and it feels like proof that you are bad at this.

It is the opposite. That reach is the workout. Every time you struggle to pull something out of memory and then check it, the memory gets stronger. Rereading skips the struggle, which is exactly why it doesn't work.

If studying feels smooth and comfortable, check whether you are studying or just visiting the material.

Rereading is not worthless. It is fine for a first pass, when the material is new. But it is a warm-up, not the workout. The moment the page starts to look familiar, close it and start retrieving.

Familiar is not known. Make your brain produce it, and it becomes yours.
