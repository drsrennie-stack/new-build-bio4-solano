A. Anterior thigh and the femoral triangle (cadaver) · Competency 127, lab · one card per numbered structure, same lines as the slide. Write the name, then fill each line as we go.

Name
1Iliopsoas
DefPsoas major and iliacus acting as one muscle, the main hip flexor
OriginPsoas major: bodies and transverse processes of T12 to L5. Iliacus: iliac fossa
InsertionLesser trochanter of the femur
ActionFlexes the hip; psoas also flexes the trunk
NervePsoas: L1 to L3 ventral rami. Iliacus: femoral nerve
Blood supplyLumbar arteries, iliolumbar artery, medial femoral circumflex
ClinicalPsoas sign: pain on passive hip extension with an inflamed appendix lying on the muscle
2Vastus intermedius
DefDeep head of the quadriceps, under rectus femoris
OriginAnterior and lateral shaft of the femur
InsertionPatella and tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament
ActionExtends the knee
NerveFemoral nerve (L2 to L4)
Blood supplyLateral femoral circumflex artery
3Vastus lateralis
DefLateral head of the quadriceps, the largest
OriginGreater trochanter and lateral lip of the linea aspera
InsertionPatella and tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament
ActionExtends the knee
NerveFemoral nerve (L2 to L4)
Blood supplyLateral femoral circumflex artery
ClinicalCommon intramuscular injection site in infants
4Vastus medialis
DefMedial head of the quadriceps, the teardrop above the knee
OriginIntertrochanteric line and medial lip of the linea aspera
InsertionPatella and tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament
ActionExtends the knee; its lowest fibers hold the patella in its groove
NerveFemoral nerve (L2 to L4)
Blood supplyFemoral artery branches
ClinicalWeak vastus medialis lets the patella track laterally (patellofemoral pain)
5Rectus femoris
DefQuadriceps head that crosses both the hip and the knee
OriginAnterior inferior iliac spine
InsertionPatella, then the tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament
ActionExtends the knee, flexes the hip
NerveFemoral nerve (L2 to L4)
Blood supplyLateral femoral circumflex artery
ClinicalStrained in sprinters and kickers; the only quadriceps head that can be torn at the hip
1Femoral nerve
DefLargest branch of the lumbar plexus
OriginL2 to L4
LocationEnters the thigh under the inguinal ligament, lateral to the femoral artery (NAVEL, lateral to medial)
SuppliesQuadriceps, sartorius, pectineus, iliacus; skin of the anterior thigh and, by the saphenous nerve, the medial leg
ClinicalInjury weakens knee extension and loses the patellar reflex
2Femoral vein
DefDeep vein of the thigh
LocationMedial to the femoral artery in the femoral triangle
ReceivesGreat saphenous vein, deep femoral vein
CourseBecomes the external iliac vein above the inguinal ligament
ClinicalSite for central venous access; a clot here is a DVT that can reach the lungs
3Femoral artery
DefMain artery of the thigh
OriginThe external iliac artery once it passes under the inguinal ligament
CourseThrough the femoral triangle between the nerve and the vein; gives the deep femoral artery; passes the adductor hiatus to become the popliteal artery
SuppliesThigh, through its deep femoral branch
BordersFemoral triangle: inguinal ligament above, sartorius lateral, adductor longus medial
ClinicalFemoral pulse below the inguinal ligament; the usual entry point for catheters

B. Gluteal region and posterior thigh (cadaver) · Competency 127, lab · one card per numbered structure, same lines as the slide. Write the name, then fill each line as we go.

Name
1Semimembranosus
DefDeep medial hamstring with a flat membranous tendon
OriginIschial tuberosity
InsertionMedial condyle of the tibia
ActionFlexes the knee, extends the hip, medially rotates the flexed leg
NerveTibial division of the sciatic nerve (L5 to S2)
Blood supplyPerforating branches of the deep femoral artery
BordersMedial edge of the popliteal fossa
2Biceps femoris
DefLateral hamstring with two heads
OriginLong head: ischial tuberosity. Short head: linea aspera
InsertionHead of the fibula
ActionFlexes the knee; long head extends the hip
NerveLong head: tibial division of the sciatic. Short head: common fibular division
Blood supplyPerforating branches of the deep femoral artery
BordersIts tendon is the lateral edge of the popliteal fossa
ClinicalHamstring strain at the ischial tuberosity in sprinters; its tendon is the landmark for the common fibular nerve at the knee
3Semitendinosus
DefSuperficial medial hamstring with a long cord-like tendon
OriginIschial tuberosity
InsertionMedial surface of the upper tibia (pes anserinus)
ActionFlexes the knee, extends the hip, medially rotates the flexed leg
NerveTibial division of the sciatic nerve (L5 to S2)
Blood supplyPerforating branches of the deep femoral artery
BordersWith semimembranosus, the medial edge of the popliteal fossa
ClinicalIts tendon is harvested for ACL reconstruction
1Piriformis
DefDeep gluteal muscle and the landmark of the gluteal region
OriginAnterior surface of the sacrum
InsertionGreater trochanter, upper border
ActionLaterally rotates the extended hip, abducts the flexed hip
NerveNerve to piriformis (S1, S2)
Blood supplySuperior and inferior gluteal arteries
BordersSuperior gluteal vessels and nerve leave above it; sciatic nerve and inferior gluteal vessels leave below it
ClinicalPiriformis syndrome: the muscle compresses the sciatic nerve, giving buttock pain down the leg
2Gluteus minimus
DefDeepest of the three gluteal muscles
OriginOuter surface of the ilium between the anterior and inferior gluteal lines
InsertionGreater trochanter, anterior surface
ActionAbducts and medially rotates the hip; keeps the pelvis level when the opposite foot is off the ground
NerveSuperior gluteal nerve (L4 to S1)
Blood supplySuperior gluteal artery
ClinicalWeak gluteus medius and minimus give the Trendelenburg sign: pelvis drops on the unsupported side
3Sciatic nerve
DefLargest nerve in the body
OriginSacral plexus, L4 to S3
CourseLeaves through the greater sciatic foramen below piriformis, runs deep to gluteus maximus and the hamstrings, splits above the knee
BranchesTibial nerve and common fibular nerve
SuppliesHamstrings, then the whole leg and foot through its branches
ClinicalSciatica from a herniated disc; an injection in the upper outer quadrant of the buttock avoids it

Name
1Tibialis anterior
DefMost medial muscle of the anterior leg
OriginLateral condyle and upper lateral tibia, interosseous membrane
InsertionMedial cuneiform and base of the first metatarsal
ActionDorsiflexes and inverts the foot
NerveDeep fibular nerve (L4, L5)
Blood supplyAnterior tibial artery
ClinicalShin splints; anterior compartment syndrome; paralysis gives foot drop
2Fibularis longus
DefSuperficial muscle of the lateral leg
OriginHead and upper two thirds of the fibula
InsertionMedial cuneiform and base of the first metatarsal, after crossing under the sole

NerveSuperficial fibular nerve (L5, S1)
Blood supplyFibular artery

3Superficial fibular nerve
DefSuperficial branch of the common fibular nerve
CourseLateral compartment, then pierces the fascia to the top of the foot
SuppliesFibularis longus and brevis; skin of the lower lateral leg and top of the foot
ClinicalInjury loses eversion; the foot tends to invert
4Soleus
DefFlat calf muscle under gastrocnemius
OriginSoleal line of the tibia, upper fibula
InsertionCalcaneus by the calcaneal tendon
ActionPlantar flexes the foot; holds you upright in standing and walking
NerveTibial nerve (S1, S2)
Blood supplyPosterior tibial and fibular arteries
ClinicalDeep veins inside it are a common site for DVT in a bedridden patient
5Extensor digitorum longus
DefAnterior leg muscle lateral to tibialis anterior
OriginLateral condyle of the tibia, upper fibula, interosseous membrane
InsertionMiddle and distal phalanges of toes 2 to 5
ActionExtends the toes, dorsiflexes the foot
NerveDeep fibular nerve (L5, S1)
Blood supplyAnterior tibial artery
6Fibularis brevis
DefLateral leg muscle under fibularis longus
OriginLower two thirds of the fibula
InsertionBase of the fifth metatarsal
ActionEverts the foot
NerveSuperficial fibular nerve (L5, S1)
Blood supplyFibular artery
ClinicalA hard inversion can pull its tendon off the fifth metatarsal (avulsion fracture)
1Anterior tibial artery
DefOne of the two end branches of the popliteal artery
CoursePasses forward through the interosseous membrane, down the anterior leg with the deep fibular nerve, onto the foot as the dorsalis pedis artery
SuppliesAnterior compartment of the leg
ClinicalDorsalis pedis pulse on top of the foot; lost in peripheral arterial disease
2Extensor digitorum longus
DefAnterior leg muscle lateral to tibialis anterior
OriginLateral condyle of the tibia, upper fibula, interosseous membrane
InsertionMiddle and distal phalanges of toes 2 to 5
ActionExtends the toes, dorsiflexes the foot
NerveDeep fibular nerve (L5, S1)
Blood supplyAnterior tibial artery
3Deep fibular nerve
DefDeep branch of the common fibular nerve
CourseRuns with the anterior tibial artery in the anterior compartment
SuppliesTibialis anterior, extensor digitorum longus, extensor hallucis longus, fibularis tertius; skin between toes 1 and 2
ClinicalDamage gives foot drop and a high-stepping gait
