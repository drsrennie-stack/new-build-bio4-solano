A Students Get Tutors

Dr. Sharilyn Rennie · Aug 24, 2026 · 3 min read

There is an idea I would really like us to get rid of.

Tutoring is for students who are struggling.

It isn't.

Somewhere along the way, many students learned that getting a tutor means you aren't smart enough, you aren't doing well enough, or you couldn't figure it out on your own.

So they wait.

They wait until they are confused. They wait until after the bad exam. They try studying harder. They tell themselves they will do better next time. And sometimes they wait until they are failing.

If you wait until you are failing, you have waited too long.

That doesn't mean you can't recover. It means you have made the job much harder than it needed to be. Now you aren't just trying to learn this week's anatomy. You are trying to learn the new material, repair what you didn't understand before, change your study habits, rebuild your confidence, and recover your grade at the same time.

That's a lot.

A students use resources

Strong students don't wait until there is a crisis to ask for help.

They ask questions. They go to office hours. They study with other people. They use tutors. They find people who understand something better than they do and learn from them.

That isn't weakness. That is strategy.

Think about almost any area where people perform at a high level. Elite athletes have coaches. Professional musicians have teachers. Physicians consult specialists. Researchers collaborate with other researchers.

Nobody watches an Olympic athlete working with a coach and thinks, "They must not be very good."

The coach is part of how they got that good.

Why should learning be different?

You don't have to be failing to get better

Maybe you understand 80 percent of the material and want help finding the other 20 percent. Maybe you're getting a B and want an A. Maybe you're already getting an A and want to make sure you actually understand the material rather than just being good at taking tests.

Maybe you want someone to quiz you. Maybe you keep confusing the same structures. Maybe you need to explain a concept out loud to someone who can tell you where the holes are. Maybe you aren't even sure whether the way you are studying is working.

Those are all good reasons to see a tutor.

Bring your notes. Bring your brain dump. Bring the structures you keep mixing up. Ask your tutor to quiz you without giving you hints. Teach the material to them and let them challenge your explanation.

A good tutor shouldn't do the learning for you. They should make you think. They should help you find weaknesses before those weaknesses show up on an exam.

Use tutoring as maintenance, not rescue

I would much rather see you working with a tutor while you have an A or B than meeting one for the first time when you are trying to rescue a failing grade.

You do not have to earn the right to ask for help by struggling first. And you don't get extra credit in life for making something unnecessarily difficult because you did it entirely by yourself.

Knowing when to use the people and resources around you is a skill.

So if you see one of the strongest students in the class sitting with a tutor, don't assume they are struggling. There is a very good chance you are looking at one of the reasons they are a strong student.

A students get tutors. Don't wait until you need rescuing. Use the resources around you to make sure you never get there.
